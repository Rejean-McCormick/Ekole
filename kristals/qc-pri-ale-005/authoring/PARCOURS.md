# Microparcours — `qc-pri-ale-005`

**Discipline (appellation québécoise) :** Anglais, langue d’enseignement  
**Intitulé éditorial :** Comprendre des textes informatifs  
**Gouvernance active :** `governance/Norme_Pedagogique_Autonome_v1.2/`  
**Statut :** contenu développé pour revue; ni certifié ni publié. La langue du gabarit YAML (`fr-CA`) est conservée; les lectures et consignes destinées à l'élève sont rédigées en anglais.

## 1. Intention et frontières

- `obj-1` — **Résultat vérifiable IMMUTABLE :** Repérer et organiser les informations d’un texte informatif en anglais.
- `crit-1` — **Preuve IMMUTABLE :** Créer une fiche de synthèse d’un document nouveau.
- **Enseignement ciblé :** distinguish a *main idea* from a *detail*, use a *heading* to anticipate or label a topic, and cite *evidence* from a text to support a summary.
- **Produit observable :** a four-part English fact sheet: relevant heading; one-sentence main idea; at least two accurate supporting details; at least one locatable quotation or paraphrase with sentence reference. It must not add unsupported facts.
- **Frontières :** no scoring on reading speed, spelling alone, personal experiences, beliefs, or background knowledge; no PFEQ equivalence claim. Use only provided original, invented documents, treated as informational texts about fictional school situations.

## 2. Notions et représentations

- **main idea:** the broad point a whole passage communicates. In “Students sort books by topic and put returned books in a basket,” the main idea is “The class has a system for organizing its books”; “a basket” is a detail, not the main idea.
- **detail:** a narrower, text-supported fact explaining or illustrating the main idea, e.g. “Returned books go in a basket.” Reject details that appear nowhere in the passage.
- **heading:** a short title that signals what the passage is mainly about; the original heading can guide reading but is not evidence that an unsupported claim is true. “Keeping Class Books Organized” is more precise than “Fun Stuff.”
- **evidence:** words or a precise paraphrase from the passage that justify a claim, with a locatable sentence number. “Sentence 2 says that books are sorted by topic” is evidence; “I think that libraries are always quiet” is not.
- **Représentations :** teacher read-aloud → sentence-numbered written text → topic/detail/evidence colour coding (also accessible by letters T/D/E) → organizer → coherent fact sheet. Concrete demonstrations and contrasts are in `contenu/01_NOTIONS_ET_EXEMPLES.md`.
- Do not copy a definition from another Kristal; prerequisite `qc-pri-ale-003` is linked, not rewritten.

## 3. Préparation / diagnostic

- **Préalable strict :** `qc-pri-ale-003` (reading fluently in English); **ordre conseillé :** aucun; **reinforces :** aucun.
- `diag-1`: distinguish an oral paraphrase from the sentence-numbered print version of *Lunch Cart*; name its main point and underline one supporting line. Answers in `contenu/02_DIAGNOSTIC.md`.
- `diag-2`: independently read *Plant Shelf* and select a heading, main idea and cited evidence. Record whether comprehension difficulty comes from decoding, vocabulary or selecting relevant information. No personal data requested.
- **Decision rule :** the reader who accurately names the main idea, identifies two text-supported details, and locates evidence in both formats can demonstrate equivalent prerequisite competence without reopening `ALE-003`; otherwise offer targeted read-aloud/fluency support or route to that prerequisite. This diagnostic is not the mastery decision for `obj-1`.

## 4. Enseignement guidé

- `teach-1`: display *The Shared Supply Shelf*. Read aloud once and let pupils reread; think aloud: “Who or what is this about? What do most sentences explain? Which details prove it?”
- Underline the recurrent concept **an organized shared shelf**, circle the specific actions (labels; return tray), reject an unsupported claim, construct a heading and write a model fact sheet with sentence evidence.
- Check comprehension: “Why is ‘a tray’ a detail rather than the main idea?” Expected: it names one component, not what the entire passage explains.
- Repeat briefly with *The Hallway Message Board*, explicitly showing that an attractive heading is not enough: source sentences must support the sheet.
- Complete English texts, think-aloud and model sheets: `contenu/01_NOTIONS_ET_EXEMPLES.md`, `contenu/03_ENSEIGNEMENT_GUIDE.md`.

## 5. Pratique accompagnée

- `guided-1`: read three different short information passages on a classroom clean-up station, a reading timetable and a seed exchange. Tasks G1/G2/G3 in `contenu/04_PRATIQUE_ACCOMPAGNEE.md`.
- **Indices dégressifs :** G1 highlights candidate details and gives an organizer; G2 supplies only questions; G3 provides no hints before the attempt. After each attempt, prompt for evidence from a specific sentence; require revision before moving on.
- **Correction:** check text fidelity, not whether the pupil's words match the model verbatim. A valid paraphrase is accepted when its meaning is supported by the text.

## 6. Pratique autonome

- `independent-1`: create individual summary sheets for four previously unseen classroom texts P1–P4 (equipment sign-out, weather-log board, reading partners and art-material sorting). All complete passages and answer keys are in `contenu/05_PRATIQUE_AUTONOME.md`.
- No teacher hint about the actual main idea during scored attempts; general tool access and a blank organizer are allowed. Evidence is written or dictated, without teacher formulation of content.
- **Separate mastery:** the two protected unseen assessment sources `qc-pri-ale-005-q-011` and `q-012` are *not* presented in teaching. They concern distinct contexts (museum label workshop and a trail-information notice). Administer separately with rubric; do not copy item text into an accessible lesson page.
- Storage of pupil work belongs to LMS/authorized environment, never to this canonical learning module.

## 7. Feedback et erreurs typiques

- **E1 — main idea is a tiny fact:** ask which idea links at least two source sentences; compare broad vs narrow choices.
- **E2 — heading copied but misleading:** ask the learner to invent a heading after rereading the whole passage; confirm with details.
- **E3 — unsupported detail:** underline the claimed fact and request its sentence; delete or replace if none exists.
- **E4 — evidence without location:** attach a sentence number or exact short quotation; do not use memory alone.
- **E5 — copying all sentences:** select two relevant details, paraphrase accurately and organize rather than reproduce the entire passage.
- Examples, corrective scripts and second attempts on different contexts are in `contenu/06_REMEDIATION_ET_TRANSFERT.md`.

## 8. Évaluation formative et preuves

- **Family:** `qc-pri-ale-005-ev-01` | **objectif_ref:** `obj-1` | **critere_ref:** `crit-1`; **type:** `réponse_argumentée`.
- **Modes techniques :** 10 fixed controlled reading stimuli and 2 scenario-controlled but fully locked sources; no free AI passage generation or undisclosed variants. See `evaluation/PLAN_EVALUATION.md` and `evaluation/BANQUE_ITEMS.json`.
- Each answer is checked for central meaning, factual support, locatable evidence and readable organization. Use `evaluation/GRILLE_MAITRISE.md` (0–2 in four dimensions) rather than a single total.
- **Mastery:** two independent fact sheets from distinct unpublished-before-assessment texts, each meeting the four criteria, including an independent evidence choice; a successful single response is insufficient.
- Status of item content: `a_revoir`, pending human language/content/rubric review and piloting.

## 9. Remédiation / enrichissement

- **Mini-lesson A:** topic vs one detail: arrange three sentence strips under a broad idea; retry a new source.
- **Mini-lesson B:** make evidence locatable: match claim cards to numbered sentences; retry without premarked sentences.
- **Mini-lesson C:** remove an invented fact: compare the student's claim with all source sentences; revise with a cited line.
- **Extension:** contrast headings and main ideas in two genres of informational text; explain why the same evidence must support the summary. Use the two original transfer tasks in `contenu/06_REMEDIATION_ET_TRANSFERT.md`.

## 10. Accessibilité

- Provide selectable plain text with sentence labels, dyslexia-friendly spacing without semantic change, assistive text-to-speech, optional teacher read-aloud, enlarged text and a tactile/verbal four-box organizer.
- Responses may be written, typed or dictated verbatim by an adult who does not choose content; count exactly the same text-grounded reasoning. Do not require colour recognition, speed or oral performance.
- If reading decoding prevents demonstration, provide authorized audio support and separately document the accommodation (not in the canonical files). Do not translate or replace the English passage with an already summarized version.

## 11. Critères de sortie

1. Independently create the **same fixed proof**: *Créer une fiche de synthèse d’un document nouveau.*
2. In **both** protected texts, identify the accurate main idea, two supporting details, source-located evidence and a readable structure; see dimension thresholds in `evaluation/GRILLE_MAITRISE.md`.
3. Show the process without content-specific prompting in at least one of the two distinct contexts; obtain a second demonstration rather than infer mastery from one score.
4. If a criterion fails, remediate the matching skill, then use a genuinely new approved text; do not recycle the failed prompt as an independent proof.
5. Schedule a short delayed retrieval (e.g., subsequent lesson and later revisit) with a fresh short factual passage, not word-for-word recall of the model.
