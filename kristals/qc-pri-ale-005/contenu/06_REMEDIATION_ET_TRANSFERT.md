# 06 — Specific feedback, remediation and transfer

## Feedback routines for common errors

| Error in a fact sheet | Text-grounded corrective question | Targeted redo (new evidence, not model copied) |
|---|---|---|
| Main idea names only a single object | “Which sentence connects at least two details?” | Highlight two source lines and write one sentence describing what both explain. |
| A heading repeats a tiny detail | “Does this heading fit most of the passage?” | Compare a detail heading with a whole-text heading; justify with two lines. |
| A detail is invented or imported from experience | “Where does the text actually say that?” | Cross out unsupported words and paraphrase a numbered line. |
| Evidence is just “I know this” | “Which sentence would another reader find?” | Add a sentence number and an exact short quote. |
| The response copies every line | “What two details matter most for your main idea?” | Condense to two supported details without changing meaning. |

Feedback script: **Notice** (“Your heading describes only the folder”) → **Locate** (“Look at sentences 1, 3 and 5”) → **Revise** (“Write a heading about the whole process”) → **Retry** (a new, shorter passage). Do not mark an unsupported interpretation as correct merely because it sounds plausible.

## Micro-remediation R1 — Broad vs narrow

Read these *invented* sentences: [1] Our class uses a card system for returning games. [2] Each game has a numbered card. [3] After play, students put the cards and games in matching boxes.

**Task:** Choose (A) “Students use cards to organize returning games,” or (B) “One card has a number” as the main idea; give evidence. **Key:** A; sentences 1 and 3 support the whole routine. B is one detail from sentence 2, and the source does not say *one particular* card only.

## Micro-remediation R2 — Locating a claim

Read: [1] At the paper display, each drawing has a title. [2] Students use the titles to find their own work. [3] A folder holds drawings that are not on display.

**Task:** Support “Titles help students find their drawings.” **Key:** Sentence 2. The folder in sentence 3 is not evidence for the title claim.

## Transfer T1 — Community Notice (informational notice genre)

> **The Neighbourhood Reading Tent — Practice Notice**  
> [1] In this fictional notice, the weekend reading tent opens in the community hall.  
> [2] Visitors sign up at a desk near the entrance.  
> [3] Volunteers show visitors where to find picture books and longer stories.  
> [4] A return table holds books after reading time.  
> [5] The notice explains how visitors can use the reading tent.

**Independent transfer task:** Make a new fact sheet about the *instructions for using the tent*. Then explain why “There is a return table” is a detail and not the main idea.

**Teacher guide:** Heading “Using the Reading Tent”; main idea “The notice explains the steps for using a community reading tent”; details signing up (2), volunteers directing visitors (3), return table (4); evidence sentence 5 for overall purpose, and sentence 2 for sign-up. “Return table” is one step rather than the entire process.

## Transfer T2 — Museum-style Exhibit Guide (instructional-information genre)

> **Labels for an Imaginary Collection**  
> [1] A fictional school exhibit displays models of unusual vehicles.  
> [2] Each model has a label giving its invented name.  
> [3] Beside the name is a sentence explaining what the model is designed to do in the story.  
> [4] Visitors follow arrows to read the labels in order.  
> [5] The labels help visitors understand and compare the imaginary designs.

**Independent transfer task:** Write a fact sheet showing *how exhibit labels inform visitors*. Give two details and cite a sentence supporting your main idea. Explain why the passage is about model labels, not real transport history.

**Teacher guide:** Heading “How the Exhibit Labels Help”; main idea “Labels and arrows guide visitors through information about imaginary vehicle models”; details names on labels (2), function descriptions (3), arrows (4); evidence sentence 5 for purpose. Real transport history is not described; the exhibit and designs are fictional.

## Delayed retrieval and follow-up decision

At a later lesson, use a fresh short English text approved by the teacher; prompt **without** supplying the main idea: “Give the central idea, two source details and one numbered piece of evidence.” Repeat later under comparable support conditions. Only record a mastery decision after the two independent *protected* contexts specified in `evaluation/GRILLE_MAITRISE.md`; transfer T1/T2 and retrieval are learning opportunities, not substitutes for unseen scored proofs.
