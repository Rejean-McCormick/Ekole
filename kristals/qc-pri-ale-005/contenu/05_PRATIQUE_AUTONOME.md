# 05 — Four independent fact sheets (practice, not protected mastery)

**Student instructions:** Read each new informational passage in English. For each, make a four-box fact sheet: (1) a useful heading, (2) one complete sentence about the main idea, (3) two accurate details, (4) evidence with a sentence number. Work without content-specific hints. A blank organizer or read-aloud of the same passage is allowed. Teacher answers appear below the four tasks; hide them until learners finish.

## P1 — Borrowing Sports Equipment

> [1] Each Friday, Class Oak borrows small play items from the classroom cupboard.  
> [2] A sign-out sheet lists each item and the group borrowing it.  
> [3] The group checks the item back in when it is returned.  
> [4] Damaged items go into a separate box for the teacher to inspect.  
> [5] These steps help everyone keep track of the shared equipment.

**Create a fact sheet.** Include the reason for the system, not just a list of sports items.

## P2 — The Weather-Observation Board

> [1] In a classroom project, students use a board to record what they notice outside.  
> [2] Each morning, they write the date and select a weather symbol.  
> [3] A space below the symbol lets them add one short observation.  
> [4] They look at earlier entries to see how their notes change during the week.  
> [5] The board keeps the class's observations together.

**Create a fact sheet.** Use only what the passage states; do not predict tomorrow's weather.

## P3 — Reading Partners

> [1] At River Classroom, students sometimes read in pairs.  
> [2] Partners take turns reading a short page aloud.  
> [3] The listening partner points to the sentence when help is needed.  
> [4] After reading, each partner shares one important idea from the page.  
> [5] These steps help both readers participate in the paired activity.

**Create a fact sheet.** Do not claim that all students read at the same speed.

## P4 — Sorting Craft Materials

> [1] Before craft projects, the class places paper pieces in containers.  
> [2] Rectangular pieces go into a box marked “rectangles.”  
> [3] Circular pieces go into a second box marked “circles.”  
> [4] Students check the shapes before choosing pieces for a collage.  
> [5] Sorting the pieces makes the collection easier to use.

**Create a fact sheet.** Give two specific source-based details.
