# 03 — Explicit lesson: read, sort, explain

**Teacher objective (read aloud):** “Today we will find a main idea, choose details that really support it, and show where the evidence comes from. Then we will organize our ideas in a fact sheet.”

## Step 1 — Activate the four questions

Show the four-box organizer without a filled answer. Ask: “What is the whole text mostly about? Which two smaller facts matter? How do I know? What short heading fits?” Explicitly distinguish the text's topic from its main idea: “Supplies” is only a topic; “The class uses a system to organize shared supplies” is an idea.

## Step 2 — I do (worked example A)

Read *A Shared Shelf* in `01_NOTIONS_ET_EXEMPLES.md` exactly as written; pupils track [1]–[5]. Think aloud:

1. “Sentence 1 introduces the shared supplies; sentences 2, 3 and 5 describe the system. That suggests the main idea.”
2. “I could choose ‘a tray holds things,’ but that describes only one part. I will write the whole-system idea.”
3. “I need **specific evidence**. I can point to sentence 2 for labels and sentence 3 for putting things away. Sentence 5 explains why the system helps.”
4. Fill the organizer in front of learners using the complete model in file 01. Underline evidence in the written passage; do not merely announce an answer.

**Check for understanding:** “Can sentence 4 alone prove that all supplies have labels?” **Answer:** No; sentence 2 says boxes have labels. “Is ‘the tray’ a good main idea?” **Answer:** No, it is a detail. Invite a different valid paraphrase supported by multiple sentences.

## Step 3 — We inspect another genre of notice

Read *Messages for Our Hallway* from file 01. Ask pupils to compare two candidate headings: *“A Blue Folder”* and *“How the Hallway Notice Board Works.”* Prompt them to say which heading fits sentences 1–5 and **why**. Write a short evidence chain: claim “The board helps students find notices” → sentence 4 (“check the board before club meetings”) → revised main idea.

## Step 4 — Guide a short student demonstration

Invite two students to propose a main idea in their own English words. Ask each to point to **two** numbered sentences that fit. Correct unsupported or overly narrow responses with *“Show me the line. What else does the passage explain?”* Avoid telling pupils that only the teacher's exact wording earns credit.

## Step 5 — Remove scaffolds

Move to G1–G3 in `04_PRATIQUE_ACCOMPAGNEE.md` (pre-highlighted choices → open questions → blank organizer). Let learners attempt G3 before showing any content hint. If accuracy remains unstable, use a targeted short exercise rather than revealing the later protected mastery texts.

**Access:** Use large type, selectable text, read-aloud or screen reader on exactly the same English sentences; colour markings must also have T/D/E letter labels. For a dictated response, the scribe records the learner's words verbatim.
