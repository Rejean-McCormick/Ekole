# 02 — Short entry diagnostic (not a mastery score)

**Administration (teacher):** Two brief tasks. Offer print and an optional neutral read-aloud of the **same English text**; record whether the learner can follow numbered sentences, identify the broad point and justify details. No stopwatch and no personal questions. A diagnostic can establish an equivalent to the `qc-pri-ale-003` prerequisite without forcing a prior course.

## D1 — Read and hear: Lunch Cart

**Student source (original fictional text):**

> **A Class Lunch Cart**  
> [1] The class keeps clean trays on a small cart.  
> [2] Before lunch, two helpers place trays on the top shelf.  
> [3] After lunch, everyone puts used trays in a marked bin.  
> [4] The cart and bin make the lunch routine easier to follow.

**Task:** Listen to a neutral reading, then read the printed source. (a) What does the whole passage mainly explain? (b) State one detail. (c) Which sentence supports the detail? (d) Explain in one short sentence whether hearing or reading helped you locate the evidence. Responses may be spoken or written.

**Teacher key:** (a) The class follows an organized tray routine at lunch. (b) Helpers put clean trays on the top shelf (sentence 2), **or** used trays go in a marked bin (sentence 3). (c) Matching number 2 or 3. (d) No “right” modality; it is an access observation, not a score.

## D2 — Independent short read: Plant Shelf

> **The Plant Shelf**  
> [1] In Room Eight, students keep three pretend paper plants on a model shelf.  
> [2] Each plant has a card with its name.  
> [3] Students use the cards to return each model to the correct place.  
> [4] The shelf helps everyone organize the models after lessons.

**Task:** (a) Choose the best heading: *“Organizing Model Plants”*, *“A Real Forest”*, or *“The Tallest Plant.”* (b) Write the main idea. (c) Give two details and one sentence number as evidence.

**Teacher key:** (a) Organizing Model Plants. (b) Students use labels and a shelf to keep model plants organized. (c) Each model has a name card (2); students use cards to return models (3); evidence sentence 2 or 3 or 4 as appropriate. Reject a claim that the paper plants are real or that any plant is tallest; the text does not say so.

## Interpretation and next step

Record separately: reading access (can follow source), broad meaning (covers the passage), factual details (two supported), and evidence location (correct sentence). When both tasks show these skills under legitimate access conditions, accept an equivalent entry skill for `ALE-003`. If reading is the obstacle, use text-to-speech / short paired rereads and an appropriate prerequisite route; if selection is the obstacle, continue with explicit topic-versus-detail teaching. Do **not** label a learner mastered in `ALE-005` using these familiar, scaffolded diagnostic passages.
