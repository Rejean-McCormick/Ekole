# 04 — Three guided practices with fading prompts

All source passages below are original fictional school situations. Work in English. Learners may use audio of the exact source and a four-box organizer. Each task asks for **heading, main idea, details and evidence**; hints deliberately decrease.

## G1 — Clean-up Station (high support)

> **Keeping the Art Table Ready**  
> [1] Room Pine has a clean-up station beside the art table.  
> [2] One basket holds used paper scraps.  
> [3] A labelled cup holds brushes that need washing.  
> [4] Students put materials in the right place after projects.  
> [5] This routine keeps the table ready for the next group.

**Prompts:** Choose the better main idea: (A) “The class has a clean-up routine for art materials” or (B) “A cup holds brushes.” Use a four-box organizer. Possible details are highlighted here: [2], [3], [4]. Find the line explaining **why** the routine helps. Write a heading.

**Model response:** Heading “The Art Table Clean-up Routine”; main idea A; details paper scraps go in a basket ([2]) and brushes go in a labelled cup ([3]); evidence [5] says the routine keeps the table ready. **Feedback:** B is true but too narrow; a main idea spans the text.

## G2 — Reading Timetable (medium support)

> **Choosing Library Times**  
> [1] The class keeps a simple reading timetable by the door.  
> [2] A different small group visits the reading corner each morning.  
> [3] Group names appear beside the days of the week.  
> [4] Students check the timetable before taking their book bags.  
> [5] The timetable helps groups take turns using the corner.

**Prompts (no highlighted lines):** What does the *whole* source explain? Which two specific details support your answer? Which sentence tells what the timetable helps students do? Make a meaningful heading and fact sheet.

**Acceptable response:** Heading “How Reading Groups Take Turns”; main idea “The reading timetable organizes when groups use the reading corner”; details a different group goes each morning ([2]); group names are listed beside days ([3]); evidence sentence [5] explains taking turns. **Feedback:** If the learner writes “Book bags are very heavy,” ask for textual evidence; the source gives none.

## G3 — Seed Exchange (minimal support)

> **Sharing Paper Seed Cards**  
> [1] For a science display, students made paper cards with drawings of imaginary seeds.  
> [2] Each card shows a shape and a made-up name.  
> [3] Pairs swap cards and sort them by shape.  
> [4] At the end, students return each card to its labelled envelope.  
> [5] This activity helps the class compare designs and keep the cards organized.

**Task:** Without hints or candidate choices, write a heading, one-sentence main idea, two supporting details and one evidence sentence with its number. Compare your draft to the rubric only **after** you finish.

**Acceptable response:** Heading “How the Class Shares Seed Cards”; main idea “Students swap and organize imaginary seed cards to compare their designs”; details cards have shape/name labels ([2]) and pairs sort by shape ([3]), or use labelled envelopes ([4]); evidence [5] directly explains why they use the activity. **Feedback:** Do not claim that real seeds grow in the envelopes: the passage explicitly describes *paper cards*.

**Fading rule:** G1 gives candidates and line suggestions; G2 gives only strategy questions; G3 gives no pre-answer hints. If a learner still needs G1-level hints during G3, return to one targeted subskill before starting independent work.
