# 01 — Informational texts: key ideas, evidence and examples

**Objective (`obj-1`):** Find and organize information from an English informational passage. **Evidence (`crit-1`):** Create a fact sheet for a new document.

## Four useful terms

1. **Main idea:** the broad message that holds the important details together. It answers, *“What does the whole text mostly explain?”* A main idea covers more than one sentence and should be supported by the passage.
2. **Detail:** a smaller fact from the text that explains, specifies or illustrates its main idea. A detail answers *“What exactly happens, where, when or how?”* It must actually be present in the source.
3. **Heading:** a short label at the top or within a text that tells a reader what subject to expect. A heading is a helpful prediction, **not proof** that every claim about the subject is true.
4. **Evidence:** words or an accurate paraphrase from a text that justify a statement. Cite the sentence number, or quote a short exact phrase and say where it appears. Prior knowledge and guesses are not source evidence.

**Check the difference:** Topic = “books.” Main idea = “Students use a shared system to keep classroom books easy to find.” Detail = “They put returned books into one basket.” Evidence = “Sentence 3 describes the return basket.”

**Reusable fact-sheet organizer** (use print, screen reader or four spoken labels):

- **Heading:** __________
- **Main idea (one complete sentence):** __________
- **Supporting details:** (1) __________ (2) __________
- **Evidence and location:** “__________” (sentence ___), or Sentence ___ says __________.

A good summary is shorter than the source; it connects the important details without inventing new information. Different wording is welcome if meaning is accurate.

## Worked example A — Shared Supply Shelf (original fictional school text)

> **A Shared Shelf**  
> [1] At Maple Room, everyone uses the same shelf for paper, pencils and folders.  
> [2] Each box has a word label and a simple picture.  
> [3] After an activity, students place unused supplies back in the matching box.  
> [4] A small tray holds materials that still need sorting.  
> [5] The system helps the class find materials without searching every desk.

**Think-aloud:** “What repeats? Sentences 1, 2, 3 and 5 all explain a shared system for finding and putting away materials. Sentence 4 gives one specific part. The whole text does not mainly explain trays.”

**Completed fact sheet:**
- **Heading:** How the Class Organizes Shared Supplies.
- **Main idea:** Maple Room uses a simple system to keep classroom supplies organized and easy to find.
- **Details:** Boxes have word and picture labels (sentence 2); students return supplies to matching boxes (sentence 3).
- **Evidence:** Sentence 5 says the system helps the class find materials without searching every desk.

**Counterexample 1 — too narrow:** “The main idea is that a tray holds materials.” **Why wrong:** sentence 4 is a single detail; it does not explain sentences 1–3 and 5. **Repair:** choose a statement about the whole shared system.

## Worked example B — Hallway Message Board (different representation)

> **Messages for Our Hallway**  
> [1] The Cedar School hallway has a board for short class notices.  
> [2] Teachers put dates at the top of each notice.  
> [3] A blue folder beside the board holds older notices.  
> [4] Students check the board before club meetings so they know where to go.  
> [5] One notice can be removed after its event has finished.

**Think-aloud:** “The heading predicts a topic, but I check the numbered lines. They explain how people post, store and use notices.”

**Completed fact sheet:**
- **Heading:** How the Hallway Notice Board Works.
- **Main idea:** The hallway board helps students find and use class notices.
- **Details:** Teachers add dates (sentence 2); students check the board before club meetings (sentence 4).
- **Evidence:** Sentence 4 directly says students check the board to learn where to go.

**Counterexample 2 — unsupported claim:** “The board sends messages to parents online.” **Why wrong:** the passage says nothing about parents, websites or messages sent online. **Repair:** replace the claim with “Older notices are placed in a blue folder” (sentence 3).

## Additional contrast: headings and evidence

A heading such as *“A Busy Hallway”* is possible, but it is vague. *“How the Hallway Notice Board Works”* better identifies what the whole passage explains. A quoted heading alone is not adequate evidence for the specific claim that teachers put dates on notices; sentence 2 is.

**Micro-check (say or write):** Is “a blue folder beside the board” a main idea or a detail? **Answer:** a detail (sentence 3). Which sentence directly supports the claim “Students use the board before meetings”? **Answer:** sentence 4.
