# Analytic mastery rubric — `qc-pri-ale-005`

**Fixed links:** `obj-1` — Repérer et organiser les informations d’un texte informatif en anglais. `crit-1` — Créer une fiche de synthèse d’un document nouveau. **Evaluation family:** `qc-pri-ale-005-ev-01`.

## Student-facing success criteria

After reading a new informative text in English, I can (1) explain what the entire text mostly tells me, (2) choose two details that really appear in the text, (3) show a numbered sentence as evidence and explain what it supports, and (4) organize these ideas in a fact sheet with a useful heading. I do not need to copy a model word for word.

## Scorer rubric — each dimension scored separately

| Dimension | 0 — Not yet shown | 1 — Developing | 2 — Independently and securely demonstrated |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Main idea / source accuracy** | Missing or conflicts with the source, or only a tiny unrelated fact. | Broad topic named but main message incomplete, overly narrow or partly unsupported. | One clear overall idea reflecting the whole text, accurate and without material invented claims. |
| **B. Relevant details** | No accurate supporting fact, or both invented. | One accurate detail, or two details with one weak/irrelevant to the stated idea. | At least two **different** accurately paraphrased details from the source that support the stated idea. |
| **C. Located evidence and reasoning** | No locatable source evidence, or quotation does not say what is claimed. | A sentence number or phrase is given but its connection to the claim is incomplete/ambiguous. | Correct sentence number and faithful quotation or paraphrase clearly support at least one substantive claim; the learner can point to why it fits. |
| **D. Organization and understandable English** | No coherent fact-sheet structure, information impossible to interpret. | Most requested parts present but confused order, imprecise heading or some meaning obscured. | An informative heading plus distinguishable main-idea/details/evidence parts; English communicates their meanings. Minor language conventions do not lower the level if meaning remains clear. |

The *full* criterion-specific model response for each passage is in `evaluation/BANQUE_ITEMS.json` under `reponse_attendue` and `corrige_structure`; it is not a compulsory exact phrase. Valid alternative wording earns the same descriptor. Do **not** judge accent, pace, handwriting, personal identity, cultural experience or spelling errors that do not affect comprehension.

## Two independent mastery demonstrations (not a single mark)

- **Proof A:** `qc-pri-ale-005-q-011`, a **fictional museum-label workshop report**, presented unseen after practice. Produce an independent fact sheet.
- **Proof B:** `qc-pri-ale-005-q-012`, a **fictional story-trail information notice**, presented unseen **on a separate occasion**. Produce another independent fact sheet.
- **Decision MAÎTRISE:** for **each** of Proof A and B, record A=2, B=2, C=2, D=2 (8/8), plus clear evidence that the learner chose the main idea/details/evidence **without hints that supplied answers**. Access support (neutral read-aloud of unchanged English, screen reader, scribe recording answers verbatim) is not disqualifying.
- **Decision EN PROGRESSION:** any missing independent proof, dimension below 2, or major unsupported claim triggers criterion-targeted re-teaching and another genuinely **new approved** unseen context. Do not average 8/8 and 4/8 to conclude mastery; do not count the same text repeated as two contexts.
- **Decision PREUVE INCONCLUSIVE:** a technical, accessibility or misunderstanding of directions problem means offer an equivalent accessible administration, not automatically assume non-mastery. Distinguish task access from source comprehension.
