# 03 — Enseignement guidé | qc-pri-ale-007

**Cible :** produire une appréciation fondée, pas seulement raconter une histoire. Déroulement indicatif : 2 séances de 20–30 minutes, ajustées selon la compréhension. Les corpus et modèles sont dans `contenu/01_NOTIONS_ET_EXEMPLES.md`.

## Séquence de modelage : “The Spare Key”

1. **Understand (literal):** Maya finds a key (line 1), considers keeping it (line 4), then gives it to the librarian (line 5). Do not guess whose key it is.
2. **Name a theme:** *taking responsibility for others' belongings* is stronger than *a key*. Ask: *What idea about people is explored?*
3. **Spot a choice:** the contrasting actions in lines 4–5 and the unexplained final light in line 6. Contrast conveys choice; the open ending invites interpretation.
4. **Select evidence:** highlight one **precise** short excerpt, e.g., *"Instead"* (line 5), in relation to line 4. A line citation must match the supplied text.
5. **Connect it:** *The contrast makes Maya's decision stand out, so I appreciate how the story shows responsibility without a lecture.*
6. **Make room for another reading:** *The note about a quiet place (line 3) might point to belonging rather than responsibility.* Respond respectfully: *That also fits, but Maya's decision is what I find most important.*

**Teacher verbalizes the test:** *If I cannot point to a line, I must soften or change my claim. I do not have to agree with another reader, but I must show I understood a plausible interpretation.*

## Second model : “The Last Kite”

- Read lines 1 and 6 aloud; invite learners to notice the contrast between expectation and feeling.
- Ask what **first person** reveals: we hear *I thought* and *I laughed*. Contrast with *A kite fell*, which would hide the narrator's reaction.
- Show that a different theme (*cooperation*) fits line 4. Neither reading requires guessing the author's personal life.
- Model the shorter paragraph: *I appreciate the first-person voice because it makes the narrator's change clear. In line 6 the narrator laughs after the kite falls in line 5. Another reading focuses on working together, supported by line 4.*

## Concept checks with answers

- *Is "a key" a theme?* **No**: it is an object/topic. *Being responsible for what we find* is a theme claim.
- *Can two interpretations both be reasonable?* **Yes**, if each is consistent with text evidence; a claim contradicted by a line is not equally supported.
- *Is a quote enough?* **No**: explain how the quotation backs up the claim.
- *If a poem says "I," must the speaker be the real author?* **No**: the speaker is the voice created by the text, unless verified external information shows otherwise.

## Discussion protocol

Teacher: *Where do you see that?* / Learner: *In line __, I notice __. This matters because __.* / Partner: *I see another possibility: __, supported by __.* / Learner: *I can accept that possibility while defending __.*

**Release of responsibility:** teacher demonstrates once, co-constructs once, then removes the claim frame and the teacher-selected line. Do not provide the mastery texts (`e1`, `e2`) during instruction. Allow enlarged print, audio reading and a bilingual glossary, but preserve the need for independent reasoning during final evidence.
