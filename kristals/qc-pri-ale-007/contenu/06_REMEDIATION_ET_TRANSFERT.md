## Transfer T1 — Editorial recommendation for a reading circle (prose)

**“The Cookbook Page” — texte original, lignes numérotées**

1. Ari copied a soup recipe from a worn family notebook.
2. The old page gave amounts but no instructions.
3. Ari tested the soup twice and wrote clear steps in the margin.
4. At the next family dinner, someone else cooked it without asking for help.
5. Ari left the notebook open on the table.

**Audience / setting:** a class reading circle choosing a small story for a shared display. **Task (English):** *Recommend or question the ending of this story for the reading circle. Name an idea the story explores, a narrative choice, one precise line and a different reasonable reading.*

**One supportable model, for corrector only:** *I recommend this ending because leaving the notebook open (line 5) suggests that knowledge is meant to be shared. The writer shows this through an action rather than a speech. Another reading could emphasize careful improvement, since Ari tested the soup twice in line 3. Both fit; the ending makes sharing most noticeable to me.*

**Criteria:** accurately distinguish copying from improving a recipe; line 4 supports independent use by someone else. A response that dislikes the ending may meet all criteria.

## Transfer T2 — Short label for a poem exhibition

**“Small Compass” — texte original, lignes numérotées**

1. A tiny needle turns in my hand,
2. though every road looks new.
3. I cannot see the whole wide land;
4. I choose one step to do.
5. The needle points; my feet decide
6. which winding path to try.

**Audience / setting:** label for a public poem display. **Task (English):** *Write or record a three-to-five-sentence appreciation for a visitor. Discuss the compass image and how the ending changes its meaning; include evidence and an alternative reading.*

**One supportable model, for corrector only:** *I like how the compass becomes a symbol of guidance without controlling the speaker. The last two lines say the needle points but the feet decide, which suggests personal choice. Another reader may focus on uncertainty, because the roads look new in line 2. The ending supports my reading because the speaker still has to choose a path.*

**Criteria:** metaphor distinguished from literal instructions; image and final contrast explained; another reading has an anchor in the poem. Do not score the student's public-speaking style or taste.

## Rappel différé and extended challenge

- **After 2–3 days:** without the frame, examine one **unseen** 3–5-line piece chosen by the teacher, and list *claim / line / effect*. Do not reuse proof texts for reteaching.
- **After 7–10 days:** revisit a prior text without the exemplar; state a different plausible appreciation supported by a different line. Compare the two claims fairly.
- **Enrichment:** compare two narrative decisions (e.g., first-person voice and open ending) without inventing authorial intent.
- **Decision:** if one proof fails, reteach only the weak dimension and administer another fixed, human-reviewed equivalent text; do not count a rehearsed model as a second independent proof.
