# 04 — Pratique accompagnée | qc-pri-ale-007

**Three exercises with fading prompts**. Present all passages in English, orally or in print. The expected paragraphs are **examples, not exclusive interpretations**. Teacher gives feedback before fading the next hint.

## G1 — Full hints: the claim, the evidence, the choice

**“The Shared Pencil” — texte original, lignes numérotées**

1. Ella brought two pencils to the drawing club.
2. Theo had none and sat before a blank page.
3. Ella slid a pencil across the table without a word.
4. Later, Theo set a bright drawing beside hers.
5. The narrator never tells us what Theo was thinking.

**Learner task:** *Do you appreciate how kindness is shown in this story? Explain the possible theme, one narrative choice, textual evidence and one alternative reading.*

**Hints available:** Theme options: *small actions can help others* or *drawing is fun*; focus on lines 2–3; narrator does not reveal Theo's thoughts (line 5). Frame: *I appreciate ... because ... Another reader might ...*.

**Model answer:** *I appreciate how the story shows kindness through a quiet action. Ella gives Theo a pencil when he has none (lines 2–3); the narrator does not need to tell us she is kind. Since Theo's thoughts are missing (line 5), another reader may interpret the final drawing as a thank-you or simply as his own enjoyment. My reading rests on Ella's action.*

**Feedback:** If learners claim Theo *must* be thankful, point to line 5: his thoughts are not given. Ask for a cautious *might*.

## G2 — One conceptual hint only

**“After the Rain” — texte original, lignes numérotées**

1. The playground puddle held a piece of sky.
2. Boots arrived, and the sky broke into rings.
3. At recess’s end, the clouds moved away.
4. The puddle held the blue again.

**Learner task:** *Write an appreciation of the poem's ending. Identify a theme, one poetic choice, one line of evidence and another plausible reading.*

**Hint (on request):** Compare lines 1 and 4: what returns, and what changes in between?

**Model answer:** *I like the repeated image of the sky in the puddle. In lines 1 and 4, the puddle holds the sky again, even after boots break its reflection in line 2. This makes change seem temporary. Another reader could focus on the fun of a busy playground; the boots in line 2 support that reading.*

**Feedback:** *The writer uses rhyming words* is unsupported: do not describe a rhyme scheme that is absent. Name the repetition and imagery instead.

## G3 — No given claim; answer with a partner, then alone

**“The Painted Sign” — texte original, lignes numérotées**

1. On Monday, the shop sign said CLOSED.
2. People passed the window without stopping.
3. On Tuesday, the owner painted a new word: RESTING.
4. A neighbour knocked and left a warm loaf at the door.
5. We never hear the owner explain the change.

**Learner task:** *Which narrative choice makes the change in the story meaningful? Defend your judgement with the text and recognize a different possible reading.*

**Teacher gives no theme or line upfront.** After the pupil's attempt, check whether a phrase or line is located accurately.

**One defensible answer:** *I appreciate the two different sign words because changing "CLOSED" to "RESTING" (lines 1 and 3) changes how the situation feels. The neighbour's visit in line 4 suggests that the softer word can invite care. Another reading might focus on how the owner is resting rather than on neighbours, because the owner gives no explanation in line 5.*

**Feedback:** A claim that the shop definitely reopened is not supported. The text never says this. Encourage *suggests / may / could*, not arbitrary inventions.

## Fading record and checkpoint

| Step | Support permitted | Evidence of readiness |
|---|---|---|
| G1 | Choice of theme, indicated lines, full frame | Connects one correct line to a claim |
| G2 | One question about repeated image | Names a poetic choice and possible effect |
| G3 | Peer discussion before solo paragraph | Independently proposes and supports a reading |

If a learner still cannot cite a line, reteach with *claim → find → explain*, not another complete model paragraph. Repeat G3 later with a fresh text so memorization of the model cannot masquerade as understanding.
