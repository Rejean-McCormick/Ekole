# 04 — Three guided reading practices, decreasing clues

**All practice:** the pupil sees English printed words, decodes them, then points to/tells a grapheme–sound clue. Neither picture nor word-recognition memory alone is a solution. Teacher documents the pupil’s actual oral reading (or agreed equivalent sound evidence), not spelling ability. Items here are **not** used in the final two mastery sets.

## G1 — Full support: short vowels in CVC words

**Card A:** `c | a | t`; **card B:** `n | e | t`. **Prompt:** “Look at the three boxes. Say each sound, blend them and read each word.”

**Scaffolds:** first, adult points and vocalizes `c` /k/, `a` /æ/, `t` /t/ once, then has pupil read *cat*. On B, adult points to each box **without saying the sounds**; pupil says /n/ /ɛ/ /t/ → *net*. Finally remove boxes or point cues and ask for the specific vowel clue. **Expected:** *cat*, `a` /æ/; *net*, `e` /ɛ/. **Feedback:** “You heard the sounds; now join them without saying letter names.” **If e and a confuse:** return to one vowel on separate practice cards.

## G2 — Partial support: identify a digraph

**Card A:** `s | i | p`; **card B:** `sh | u | t` with `sh` underlined. **Prompt:** “Read the words. Tell me what the underlined letters do.”

**Scaffolds:** teacher only underlines `sh`, does not pronounce it. Pupil reads *sip* /sɪp/ and *shut* /ʃʌt/; tells or points to `sh` /ʃ/ (two letters for one sound). On second round, underline is removed, and pupil rereads without sound hints. **Feedback:** “Your `s` made /s/. In `sh`, the letters work as one grapheme; try its sound.”

## G3 — Minimal support: unseen at first exposure within teaching

**Cards:** `cut` and `chat` in ordinary print, no boxes. **Prompt:** “Read both words and show the spelling clue for one sound in each.” Adult says nothing until the two readings are completed.

**Expected:** *cut* /kʌt/ with `u` /ʌ/; *chat* /tʃæt/ with `ch` /tʃ/ (or `a` /æ/). **Allowed:** one silent self-correction initiated by the learner; any established English accent yielding recognizable words. **Feedback:** “You kept `ch` together and blended left to right”; if needed, **after** the attempt contrast `ch` and `c` on separate tiles.
