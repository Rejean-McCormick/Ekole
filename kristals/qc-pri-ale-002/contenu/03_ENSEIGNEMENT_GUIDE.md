# 03 — Explicit modelling: from grapheme to spoken word

**Learning target for students:** “I can read a new regular word by looking at its letters and spelling patterns, saying the sounds, blending them, and showing the clue I used.”

## Step 0 — Set up a non-guessing task

Write `map` with three boxes `m | a | p`. Cover any illustrations and pronounce English sample phonemes accurately; do not insist on a single national accent. Choose contrastive vowel patterns separately before mixing them (e.g. learn `a` /æ/ before comparing with `e` /ɛ/).

## Step 1 — Teacher think-aloud (exactly one full model)

1. “I look from **left to right**.” Point to `m`, `a`, `p`.
2. “`m` spells /m/. `a` spells /æ/ in this word. `p` spells /p/.”
3. “I blend them without extra vowel sounds: /m/ … /æ/ … /p/ → **map**.”
4. “My clue is the letter `a`; it gives the short vowel /æ/ here. The other letters confirm the word.”

Then compare `b | e | d` → /b/ /ɛ/ /d/ → *bed*. Ask: “Which middle grapheme changed? What sound changed?” **Answer:** `a` versus `e`, /æ/ versus /ɛ/; the word changes accordingly.

## Step 2 — Two letters, one sound

Introduce the already-taught sequence `sh | u | t`. Say “`sh` works together to spell /ʃ/.” Show three sound boxes for three phonemes; read **shut** /ʃʌt/. Then `ch | a | t` → **chat** /tʃæt/. Explain `sh` and `ch` as distinct digraphs rather than decomposing them into letter-name sounds.

## Step 3 — Compare the blend meanings

The English word **blending** in this lesson names the **reading process**: `/s/ + /ɪ/ + /p/ → sip`. A **consonant blend** is a consonant cluster, such as `sl` in *slip*, in which **both** /s/ and /l/ can be heard. `sh` in *shut* is a **digraph**, not a two-sound consonant blend. Do not score knowledge of the advanced label when the pupil can show the actual correspondences.

## Step 4 — Error analysis without public correction pressure

| Observed attempt | Teacher response | Expected retry |
|---|---|---|
| Reads `m` as its letter name “em” while sounding the word | “That is its name. What sound does it spell here?” | /m/ |
| Says */pə/* for `p` | “Say the clean consonant sound, then move to the next sound.” | /p/ blended into the vowel |
| Splits `sh` into /s/ and /h/ | “Circle both letters in one box. What is that one sound?” | /ʃ/ |
| Guess from picture | “Hide the picture; which grapheme supports the sound?” | Printed evidence and read word |

## Quick check before guided practice

**Teacher asks:** “What is a grapheme? Which part of *shut* shows one sound with two letters? What do we do when we blend sounds?” **Expected:** a spelling of one sound; `sh`; join sounds in reading order. This oral/text explanation is only a learning check; mastery later requires decoding **new written words**.
