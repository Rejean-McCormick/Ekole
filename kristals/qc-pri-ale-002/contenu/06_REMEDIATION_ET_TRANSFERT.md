# 06 — Targeted repair, two transfers and spaced retrieval

## Feedback on typical mistakes

| Observed result | Likely difficulty to test, not diagnose | Intervention | Fresh criterion check |
|---|---|---|---|
| Names `m, a, p` but never reads *map* | Mixing letter names and phonemes | Adult contrasts letter **name** “em” and **sound** /m/, then models phoneme joining. | Read `mat` without name prompts; say what `a` spells. |
| Adds a vowel after /t/ or /p/ | Speech segmentation distorting blending | Stretch a continuous sound (/m/ or /s/) and shorten the stop; join immediately. | Independently read `tap` and cite `a` /æ/. |
| Reads `sh` as two phonemes | Grapheme width not identified | Mark `sh` in one tile; contrast with `sl` as two sounds. | Read `mesh`, identify `sh` /ʃ/. |
| Reads `i` as long /aɪ/ in a controlled CVC word | Wrong vowel correspondence for chosen pattern | Re-model short `i` /ɪ/ with `sit`, then remove example. | Read `lip` and point to `i`.
| Gives a correct word but cannot justify it | Memorized word or guess could mask process | “Which grapheme gave the middle sound?” Avoid giving the answer. | New regular word, show one evidence cue. |
| Accurate correspondence with unfamiliar accent | Accent difference rather than code error | Accept valid variants; review only a sound contrast actually needed for distinguishing the target. | Confirm clue and blending, not imitation of a teacher's accent. |

### Transfer T1 — Science shelf cards

**Situation:** a school science activity has fictional labels in a drawer, not pictures. The pupil finds `pet` and `fish`. **Instruction:** “Read each label and show the spelling clue for the vowel or the final sound.”

**Locked correctors:** `pet` /pɛt/, middle `e` = /ɛ/; `fish` /fɪʃ/, last `sh` = /ʃ/. **Why transfer:** decoding is used to interpret labels rather than isolated phonics lesson cards. **Feedback:** if *fish* is read as /fɪs h/, regroup `sh` into one tile **after** the first attempt.

### Transfer T2 — Invented story captions

**Situation:** in a teacher-created picture-free story notebook, two English words are printed: `mud`, `chat`. **Instruction:** “Read the two words, then name the letter group you relied on.”

**Locked correctors:** `mud` /mʌd/, `u` /ʌ/; `chat` /tʃæt/, `ch` /tʃ/. **Why transfer:** short caption context versus labelled objects; no illustration or sentence context supplies the answer. **Feedback:** if the pupil names `c-h` without recognizing one /tʃ/, show the one-graphème/two-letter tile during remediation.

## Delayed retrieval

- **Next lesson:** retrieve two trained correspondences on `cat` and `cut`, mixed order, without a model; ask one grapheme/sound clue each.
- **A few lessons later:** offer one new but untaught, non-mastery practice word, `nap`, plus `mesh`; collect readings and named evidence. Expected *nap* /næp/ (`a` /æ/), *mesh* /mɛʃ/ (`sh` /ʃ/). Never use the eight reserved words of final proofs as a drill.
- **If unresolved:** revisit only the error family, then test with a newly screened word. Avoid copying finished answers as a substitute for decoding.

## Equity and limits

Speech articulation, hearing differences, reading accommodations, limited English lexicon and anxiety may affect the **measurement**. Keep printed decoding separate from picture comprehension and vocabulary knowledge. An adult chooses an equivalent audio/phoneme selection method in advance if voice is inaccessible; software must not perform recognition/reading on behalf of the pupil. Do not interpret error counts as diagnoses.
