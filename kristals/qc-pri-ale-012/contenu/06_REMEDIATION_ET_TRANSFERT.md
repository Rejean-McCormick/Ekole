# 06 — Targeted feedback, new contexts, deferred recall

## Common errors and a precise intervention

| Error observed | Feedback in English | Small next task / evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Polishing grammar while the story is out of order | *“Your sentences are readable, but I still cannot follow the event order. Put the events on a timeline first.”* | Arrange three new event cards; justify the order; proofread **after**. |
| Inventing missing reasons or details | *“Which words in the original prove that? If none do, ask the writer instead.”* | Annotate unknown fact with `?` and draft an English question; retain only established facts. |
| Unclear `it`, `they` or `she` | *“Who or what does this pronoun name? Show the noun.”* | Draw pronoun → antecedent arrows; if two are possible, ask for clarification. |
| Inaccurate link, e.g. `because` instead of `after` | *“Does the draft show a reason or only an order?”* | Sort *then/after/because/however* by relation; choose only an evidenced link. |
| No annotations or vague `I made it better` | *“Name your before-and-after words and the reader's benefit.”* | Fill one `Before → After → Because` row, then repeat without the template. |
| Conventions remain unclear | *“Read each sentence after you are satisfied with the meaning.”* | Sentence-by-sentence end punctuation, sentence capitals, verb tense check. |

## T1 — Transfer: instruction for organising a display (new procedural context)

**Independent draft (fictional):** `Finally, put the labels beside the drawings. First, choose two drawings. put the drawings on the board. Then look at the board.`

**Reader/purpose:** a pupil setting up a classroom display. **Task:** *Produce a revised set of instructions and an annotated before/after explanation. Use only the stated actions.*

**Possible revision:** `First, choose two drawings. Then put the drawings on the board. Next, look at the board. Finally, put the labels beside the drawings.`

**Annotations and rationale:** `[M]` arrange the four actions into the indicated first–then–finally sequence; `[C]` make the transitions explicit without changing the stated last action; `[F]` capitalise `Put` if that sentence remains, or use correctly capitalised `Then`. The original *Finally* still marks putting the labels in place; no new materials or activity are added. Alternative orders with a logically defensible purpose may be accepted, but all four named actions must remain.

## T2 — Transfer: polite email to a club volunteer (new audience)

**Independent draft (fictional):** `Hello, I have two questions about the club meeting. I have two questions about the club meeting. Is the meeting on Tuesday? where should we meet? Thank you, Sam`

**Reader/purpose:** a club volunteer receiving a clear request. **Task:** *Improve clarity without pretending you know the volunteer's answers. Show the annotations and reasons.*

**Possible revision:**
> Hello,
>
> I have two questions about the club meeting. Is it on Tuesday? Where should we meet?
>
> Thank you,  
> Sam

**Annotations:** `[M]` remove repeated sentence; `[C]` group two questions together for the reader; `[F]` `where` → `Where` and format the greeting/closing. **Do not** invent a meeting time or place. Accept other polite formats with the same questions.

## Independent mastery versus transfer practice

T1 and T2 are **optional transfers** for practice. The two **formal** new-context proofs are `qc-pri-ale-012-q-011` (fictional narrative) and `qc-pri-ale-012-q-012` (informational message), assessed separately and without item-specific hints using `evaluation/GRILLE_MAITRISE.md`. Neither a transfer worksheet nor a single high score substitutes for these two proofs.

## Retrieval after approximately seven days (planning suggestion, not a proven optimal interval)

Show without the notes: `First, the team reached the park. then the team checked the map. First, the team reached the park.` Ask for a two-pass revised version and one reason for each pass. **Possible:** `First, the team reached the park. Then the team checked the map.` `[M]` remove repeated information; `[F]` `then` → `Then`. Ask students to recall the order **meaning → cohesion → conventions**. Record what was remembered; reteach the weakest stage, then use a different check later. No child's personal experiences are required.
