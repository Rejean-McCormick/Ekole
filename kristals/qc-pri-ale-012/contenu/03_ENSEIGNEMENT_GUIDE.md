# 03 — Explicit guided teaching (one short lesson, ~20–25 minutes as an optional estimate)

## Launch — Reader first

Teacher says: *“Today we are editors. A reader should understand our message. We will fix **meaning** first, then **connections**, and finally **conventions**. We will show why each change helps.”* Display the target: *“I can submit an annotated, improved English draft and explain my choices.”*

Ask learners to read this invented note aloud or with text-to-speech: `Friday is our club day. bring your ideas. Friday is our club day.` Ask: *“Is spelling the biggest problem?”* Expected: No—the repeated information blocks the message; perhaps the reader also needs a location, **but we must ask rather than invent one**.

## Think-aloud — The garden report

Use Worked Example 1 in `01_NOTIONS_ET_EXEMPLES.md`. The teacher points to each sentence, then thinks aloud:

1. *“Who will read it? A classmate. What are the three facts already stated? Three raised beds, planting on Tuesday, and daily watering.”* Write facts on cards, not on the final copy yet.
2. *“I see the same fact twice: three beds. I can remove the second mention.”* Mark `[M] repeated detail removed`.
3. *“Does 'They' mean beds or seeds? I must ask the writer. The writer confirms 'seeds'. Now I can write 'The seeds'.”* Mark `[C] clearer reference`.
4. *“The reader may follow the event more easily if I begin 'On Tuesday'. I still tell the same event.”* Mark `[C] flow`.
5. *“Only now do I check sentence capitals.”* Mark `[F] we → On Tuesday, our class ...`.
6. Read revised report; compare meaning to original **line by line**. Explain that *garden* is not a new fact and *seeds* is confirmed.

## Think-aloud — The key search

Use Worked Example 2. Place story cards in narrated order, then in event order. Ask: *“Does 'At last' belong at the start of this story?”* The writer confirms the key was inside the cupboard. Reorder, connect using *Then*, change `open` to `opened`. Show `[M]`, `[C]`, `[F]` marks on the page, then give an alternative: `Jo first looked under the desk. Next, Jo opened the cupboard and found the key inside.` Both keep the same events.

## Check for understanding (responses plus feedback)

- **Q1:** *“Can I add a reason that the author never gave?”* **No.** Ask for clarification or keep the statement as it is.
- **Q2:** *“What should I fix first: `we` → `We`, or an event told backwards?”* **The order.** Readers need correct meaning first.
- **Q3:** *“Is 'because' always an improvement?”* **No.** It asserts a cause. Use it only when the draft supports a reason.
- **Q4:** *“If an English sentence is already easy to understand in a legitimate variety of English, must I erase its voice?”* **No.** Check whether it meets the purpose and audience rather than marking difference as error.

## Exit ticket before guided practice

Draft: `First we packed. Finally we arrived. Then we boarded the bus.` Task: *“Choose the most important first change and justify it.”* Expected sequence: `First we packed. Then we boarded the bus. Finally we arrived.` Mark `[M]` and optionally `[C]`; the sentence punctuation can be checked afterward. Do not mark a pupil wrong for an equally coherent solution preserving these three events.
