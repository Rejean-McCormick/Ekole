# 04 — Three guided exercises with fading prompts

**How to work:** For every exercise, mark `[M]` (meaning), `[C]` (cohesion) and `[F]` (conventions) where relevant, produce a better English version, then complete *“I changed ___ because ___.”* Do not invent a fact. The teacher gives less help each time. All contexts and drafts are fictitious.

## G1 — Remove repetition in a club reminder (strong scaffolding)

**Original:** `The art club meets after lunch. The art club meets after lunch. we bring paper.`

**Reader:** art club members. **Purpose:** clear reminder. **Help card:**

1. Underline the idea that is repeated word for word.
2. Put `[M]` above the unnecessary copy and remove it.
3. Find a sentence that begins with a lowercase letter and mark `[F]`.
4. Keep the paper information. Write one `because` explanation about clarity.

**One acceptable revision:** `The art club meets after lunch. We bring paper.`

**Annotations:** `[M]` deleted duplicate meeting sentence because the reader needs the information once; `[F]` `we` → `We` because it starts a sentence. **Feedback:** If the learner changes the meeting time, restore *after lunch*; revision must preserve the given fact. No fabricated location is permitted.

## G2 — Restore sequence (partial scaffolding)

**Original:** `Finally, Eli hung the poster. First, Eli wrote the title. then Eli drew a picture.`

**Reader:** classmates reading a process report. **Prompts (no answer highlighted):**

- Which action happened first, next and last according to the draft's sequence words?
- Which connector must be relocated, and which convention should be checked **after** ordering?
- Use `[M]`, `[C]` and `[F]` as needed and justify two changes.

**One acceptable revision:** `First, Eli wrote the title. Then Eli drew a picture. Finally, Eli hung the poster.`

**Annotations:** `[M/C]` moved the final event last for chronological clarity; `[F]` `then` → `Then` for a sentence opening. **Feedback:** A grammatically polished draft that still starts with *Finally* has not repaired the primary meaning/order problem.

## G3 — Clarify a report (minimal scaffolding)

**Original:** `Our group made a short guide for new students. it has a map. New students need a map. We gave the guide to new students yesterday.`

**Task without a hint list:** *“Revise for a reader. Keep all distinct facts. Mark each change and explain its benefit. Read your copy aloud.”*

**One acceptable revision:** `Our group made a short guide for new students. The guide has a map, which new students need. Yesterday, we gave the guide to them.`

**Notes:** `[C]` `it` → `The guide` identifies the antecedent; the map fact is linked to why it matters; `[C]` `Yesterday` leads the final sentence; `[F]` `it` begins a sentence in the original and must be capitalized if kept. Other clear rephrasings that preserve **guide, new students, map, need for map, yesterday's handover** earn credit. Avoid adding an unreported school location.

**Fading check:** G1 teacher points; G2 teacher asks but does not move cards; G3 teacher waits for independent proposal, then gives descriptive feedback. Learners needing hints after G3 repeat a new short case with one prompt, rather than receiving the final answer to copy.
