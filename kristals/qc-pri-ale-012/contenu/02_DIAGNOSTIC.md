# 02 — Two short diagnostic tasks (not mastery proofs)

**Teacher note:** No prerequisite beyond `qc-pri-ale-011`. The learner may demonstrate an equivalent ability to plan and produce short English texts here; a weak result triggers a short support task, not automatic exclusion. Give these drafts before teaching the revision codes. Allow oral reasoning or text-to-speech; capture decisions, not reading speed.

## D1 — A notice with a missing fact (about 4 minutes)

**Fictional draft:** `Our club meets on Thursday. Please come to the club. we meet on Thursday. Bring it.`

**Task:** *Who is the reader? What is one repeated idea? What question must you ask the writer before replacing “it”? Correct one writing convention. Explain what you fixed first.*

**Observable expected answer:** A club member is the likely reader; `meets on Thursday` repeats; ask *“What should members bring?”* (the object has no stated antecedent); `we` at the beginning of a sentence must be `We`. Do not decide that `it` means a bag or book. Prioritise repeated/unclear meaning, then a capital letter.

**Decision:** Accept an equivalent question; treat an invented item as evidence of a need for [M] clarification rather than a failure of spelling. May orally explain with annotations.

## D2 — Ordering events (about 5 minutes)

**Fictional draft:** `Finally, the kite landed. at first, Nia ran with the kite. Then the kite flew above the field.`

**Task:** *Put the three events in a helpful order, correct the capital letter, and say why you worked on order before the capital.*

**Expected revision:** `At first, Nia ran with the kite. Then the kite flew above the field. Finally, the kite landed.` Explanation: events must make sense in sequence before polishing the capital of `At`.

## Routing, recorded by the educator

- **Ready:** D1 identifies the unresolved referent and D2 orders the three events and explains the revision order → proceed; this diagnostic can attest equivalent entry skill when `qc-pri-ale-011` was not formally taken.
- **Partly ready:** one task satisfactory → use sentence cards, display the reader's question and model a two-sentence draft before `G1`.
- **Needs preparation:** neither task satisfactory → have the learner plan a three-sentence note (reader/purpose/sequence), linked to the capability of `qc-pri-ale-011`; re-diagnose with new sentences.
- **Caution:** These two tasks do **not** establish mastery of `obj-1`, which requires annotated, justified revisions in **two independent genres**.
