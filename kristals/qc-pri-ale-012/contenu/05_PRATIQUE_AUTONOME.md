# 05 — Four independent practices, with model responses

**Instructions to students (English):** Read the whole draft. Decide who will read it and why. Revise **meaning and organization first**, then links between ideas, then grammar and punctuation. Submit (1) the original with `[M]`, `[C]`, `[F]` marks, (2) your clean revision and (3) at least **two** reasons, one about meaning/cohesion and one about form. Ask a question if an essential fact is unclear; **do not fill it in with a guess**. Work without content suggestions from the teacher. The model answers below are released **after** collection. A good alternative version may be accepted.

## A1 — How-to card (sequence)

**Draft:** `Finally, cut along the line. First, draw a shape on the paper. then colour the shape.`

**Reader/purpose:** another pupil following a craft instruction. **Your challenge:** reorder the three steps, check the sentence openings, annotate and explain.

**Model after submission:** `First, draw a shape on the paper. Then colour the shape. Finally, cut along the line.`

**Rationale:** `[M/C]` move *cut* to the end so a reader can follow the process; `[F]` `then` → `Then`. Correctness depends on preserving the original three steps, not on exact phrasing.

## A2 — Short newsletter note (repetition and cohesion)

**Draft:** `Our hallway has a new reading bench. The bench is near the door. our hallway has a new reading bench. Students can sit there during reading time.`

**Reader/purpose:** school newsletter readers. **Your challenge:** keep distinct facts, remove needless repetition, and repair a convention.

**Model:** `Our hallway has a new reading bench near the door. Students can sit on the bench during reading time.`

**Rationale:** `[M]` remove a duplicated fact; `[C]` connect the location to the bench and clarify *there*; `[F]` the original `our` starts a sentence and would require `Our` if retained. A learner who deletes that entire duplicated sentence rather than capitalizing it has removed the form error; **do not demand an extra artificial correction**. To show `[F]`, the learner can identify and justify a convention checked (no change required). The facts about the new bench, hallway, door and reading time stay intact.

## A3 — Short story (order and verb forms)

**Draft:** `Finally, Tessa won the race. First Tessa tied her shoe. Tessa run around the track. She cross the finish line.`

**Reader/purpose:** a class story. **Your challenge:** place events in order, connect them, then correct past-tense forms and punctuation.

**Model:** `First, Tessa tied her shoe. Then Tessa ran around the track. She crossed the finish line and won the race.`

**Rationale:** `[M]` the win follows crossing the finish line; `[C]` *Then* joins the events; `[F]` `run` → `ran`, `cross` → `crossed`, and comma after `First`. Do not invent another racer or finishing time.

## A4 — Science journal excerpt (clear observation versus unsupported cause)

**Draft:** `A shadow appeared under the lamp. Our class moved the paper closer to the lamp. The shadow became bigger. becuase.`

**Reader/purpose:** a reader of a classroom observation. **Your challenge:** write the sequence clearly and repair the incomplete last word, without claiming a proven cause that the draft does not establish.

**Model:** `A shadow appeared under the lamp. Our class moved the paper closer to the lamp. After the move, the shadow became bigger.`

**Rationale:** `[M]` remove dangling `becuase.` (it lacks a reason); `[C]` *After the move* accurately places the observation in time without asserting causality; `[F]` if preserving *because*, its spelling would be `because`, but it needs a complete reason. Removing the unfinished fragment is the stronger repair. The observation remains a **fictional account**, not a tested science explanation.

## After the four tasks

For each A1–A4, self-rate these questions (yes / not yet): *Did I preserve what the author actually said? Did I improve sense or order? Do the sentences connect truthfully? Did I check conventions last? Did I explain at least two changes?* Reattempt a different prompt in `06_REMEDIATION_ET_TRANSFERT.md` if any answer is “not yet”. These four exercises are **practice, not the two required independent mastery proofs**.
