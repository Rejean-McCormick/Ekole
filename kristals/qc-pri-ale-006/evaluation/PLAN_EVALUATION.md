## Response evidence and criterion-based judgement

For each target inference, collect a triplet: **(1) distinct implied idea** compatible with full passage, **(2) exact textual phrase(s) or faithful paraphrase, (3) a warranted “because” explanation.** A direct copy without inference, a guess relying only on personal opinion, or a contradiction is insufficient. English should be intelligible but minor grammar/spelling errors must not erase a sound reading inference. Quote only the short relevant words, not a whole passage.

- **Initial level (001–004):** one precise inference and a truthful clue–warrant connection; acknowledge a limit where asked.
- **Intermediate (005–008):** combine or compare details, distinguish unstated intent from fact and resist overconfidence.
- **Transfer (009–010):** defend two *different* inferences with separate evidence and explicit reasons, in less familiar formats.
- **Proof A (011):** narrative, goal/response over time; two valid distinct chains, without scoring-time prompts or exemplar visible.
- **Proof B (012):** factual-style fictional library notice; two valid distinct chains about separate organizer intentions; different genre and reasoning pattern from A.

**Rubric dimensions:** validity/nonliteral nature; appropriate evidence; reasoned connection; distinction/limits; autonomy and transfer. See `evaluation/GRILLE_MAITRISE.md` for four levels (0–3) and the mastery decision. **Two proofs, two genres, two inferences per proof**; successful completion of a single item or a total percentage alone does not imply mastery. An alternative inference is creditable when (a) not already explicitly stated, (b) text-consistent, (c) supported by specific clues, and (d) persuasively connected in the learner’s explanation. It need not match the exemplar’s wording.

## Variability and secure administration

- **All 12 items:** fixed stimuli, fixed difficulty, fixed instructions, `seed: null`, `parametres: null`. No free generation or automatic swapping. Allowed *learner* variability: oral/written answer, exact quotation or faithful paraphrase, multiple text-warranted interpretations. **Not permitted:** teacher changing the evidence, adding a line, identifying the correct clue during a proof, guessing the unprovided intentions of characters, or using an unverifiable source.
- **Administration:** show just the stimulus and consigne (never the bank answer or feedback), keep rubric for assessor, remove solved exemplars from view, do not reuse the teaching passages for mastery. Record assistance; audio reading of the *same* text may be provided without expressive cues emphasizing “correct” clues.
- **Safety and equity:** invented neutral situations, no private disclosures and no real personal data; never infer protected identity, family situations, morality or medical diagnosis. Any depiction of dog discomfort does not ask children to administer care; it is a reading task. Keep passages accessible in readable text and audio with identical content. Allow dictated response if justified by access needs, without giving interpretation hints.
- **Human control before use:** editor/English language reviewer checks ambiguity of every inference, difficulty for intended readers, cultural fairness, passage comprehensibility, and scoring consistency with several plausible learner formulations; educational team conducts pilot/consistency review before summative use. No automated grader or AI-generated “answer key” is treated as infallible.

## Error→feedback→next evidence

If a student invents a cause or exact feeling, cite the relevant line and ask which claim goes beyond it; if the learner merely copies a line, explain explicit vs implicit; if an answer lacks the bridge, reteach the connection; if two inferences repeat, elicit a different possible aim from a *new* text. Give corrective feedback **after** an assessment attempt, not while gathering independent evidence. For failed A or B, teach with other passages and collect replacement **reviewed** evidence in the missing genre rather than retesting memorized answers.
