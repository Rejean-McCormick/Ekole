# Criterion rubric — independent mastery for `qc-pri-ale-006`

**Objective:** `obj-1` — Inférer une intention ou une information non explicite dans un texte anglais.  
**Criterion:** `crit-1` — Formuler deux inférences soutenues par des indices textuels.  
**Evaluation family:** `qc-pri-ale-006-ev-01` (`réponse_argumentée`).

## Rate each inferred idea separately

| Dimension | 0 — Missing | 1 — Emerging | 2 — Satisfactory | 3 — Strong / transferable |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **I: inference accuracy** | No inference or contradicts passage | Literal statement, unsupported or overconfident guess | Unstated, plausible and consistent with text | Plausible, nuanced; distinguishes alternatives or limits |
| **E: text evidence** | None or invented | Broad reference, misplaced or inaccurate detail | One or more **specific accurate** textual clues supporting claim | Well-chosen multiple clues, faithfully situated and compared |
| **J: justification** | No link | “Because” without relevant reasoning / merely repeats claim | Explains **why** cited words support conclusion | Clear causal/interpretive reasoning and checks other possibility |
| **L: limits and distinction** | Invents facts or repeats same idea twice | Some overreach, duplicated inference or uncorrected contradiction | Does not add facts; two genuinely different inferences where required | Explicitly calibrates uncertainty; discriminates between strong/weak claims |

**Minimum satisfactory inference:** levels **I ≥ 2, E ≥ 2, J ≥ 2** and no fabricated or text-contradicting claim; `L ≥ 2` evaluated across the pair. A quoted fact alone (even perfectly spelled) fails I; a reasonable hunch without evidence fails E. Scoring is analytic for diagnostic feedback, **not** a mechanical average.

## Evidence conditions

- **Proof A — `qc-pri-ale-006-q-011`:** unfamiliar short narrative, two **distinct** unstated claims plus accurate supporting clues and explanations. Student may choose different valid interpretations than examples; record why scoring is defensible.
- **Proof B — `qc-pri-ale-006-q-012`:** unfamiliar fictional information notice; two **distinct** intentions or implied information, with evidence and reasoning. Independent genre transfer, no reuse of evidence or conclusion from A.
- **Autonomy:** no teacher identification of clue, no answer options, no guided sentence that reveals an interpretation. Allow faithful read-aloud, accessible transcription and non-leading prompts repeated verbatim. Note every support; if support steers inference, mark that attempt *assisted*, reteach and reassess with fresh reviewed materials.
- **Timing:** conduct B at a different sitting when feasible (e.g., next lesson), avoiding immediate mimicry. Dates and actual conditions are recorded in LMS, not here.

## Mastery decision (not a single numeric score)

**Mastery demonstrated:** both A and B show at least **two** different satisfactory inferences **each**, with I/E/J at ≥2 per inference, L≥2 for the pair, and documented independence; both passages clearly understood and no material contradictions. Four inference chains across two genres are required, not simply a sum of 8/12 points or a perfect response in one genre.

**Needs targeted remediation:** if an inference is literal, unsupported, loosely connected, redundant, or conflicts with the text, identify which dimension fails. Teach on a **different** small passage: stated-vs-inferred sort (I), precise underlining (E), because bridge (J), or limit/contrast (L). Require a new independent demonstration in the affected genre after feedback and human review; do not edit scored work retroactively into a passing attempt.

**Not yet evaluated:** missing passage, inaccessible format, reader unable to decode the source and not offered allowable support, or administration with clue-giving help. Never code this as “failed comprehension” without more evidence.

## Fairness and language

Score *understanding of an English passage* and clear interpretive justification, not accent, handwriting speed or minor surface grammar. Correct major misunderstandings of text vocabulary before assessment using a different example, not by rephrasing the target evidence during scoring. Readers may explain orally or in writing in intelligible English. The evaluator records alternatives, uncertainties and reasons for any difficult judgement. Grille et items: **a_revoir** until human calibration; no certification claim.
