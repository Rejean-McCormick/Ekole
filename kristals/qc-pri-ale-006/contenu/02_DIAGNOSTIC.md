# 02 — Diagnostic: literal comprehension, inference, evidence

**Purpose:** locate support needs before teaching; do not use as the two final mastery demonstrations. Administer orally and in writing to compare barriers. Do not assume the required `qc-pri-ale-004` must be repeated if equivalent reading comprehension is demonstrated.

## D1 / `diag-1` — Oral passage (teacher reads exactly twice if requested)

> The breakfast table was still set. Evan looked at the clock and pushed his toast aside. “I can eat the rest later,” he said. He picked up his backpack and moved quickly toward the door.

**Questions to ask, without coaching:**
1. What food does Evan push aside? Give a **stated fact**.
2. What can we infer about Evan's immediate priority? Name two words or actions that help you decide.
3. Is “Evan dislikes toast” stated or safely inferred? Explain.

**Model response:** (1) His toast. (2) He may be trying to leave soon / reach another obligation on time: he *looks at the clock*, *says he can eat later*, and *moves quickly toward the door*. (3) No; dislike is unsupported. **Accept** another explanation consistent with those clues; do not require “school,” which is absent.

## D2 — Written passage (student receives identical print)

> **Art workshop notice:** Doors open at 2:45 p.m. The first activity begins at 3:00 p.m. Please have your materials ready when you arrive. Extra paper and pencils will be available at the welcome table.

**Questions:**
1. When does the activity begin (stated fact)?
2. Why might the organizers ask participants to prepare materials on arrival? Support your inference with a clue.
3. Could we conclude that the workshop charges an entry fee? Why or why not?

**Model response:** (1) 3:00 p.m. (2) They may want the group to begin promptly: the notice says materials should be ready when people arrive, and the activity starts at a fixed time. (3) No, there is no mention of any fee. “Extra paper and pencils” does **not** imply a fee.

## Quick observation grid (not a graded exam)

| Evidence | Present independently | Needs oral / print support | Not yet visible |
|---|---|---|---|
| Identifies stated details accurately | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |
| Proposes a plausible **unstated** idea | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |
| Points to or paraphrases a real clue | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |
| Explains a link and recognizes limits | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |

**Branching:** If D1 succeeds and D2 fails, offer decoding, segmentation or read-aloud while keeping clues identical. If both fail on literal facts, practise brief narrative comprehension before this module (link `qc-pri-ale-004`, or diagnose equivalence anew). If literal facts succeed but inferences fail, teach the C.L.U.E. chain. If inferences are present but unsupported, rehearse the “because + exact words” step. Diagnostic does not certify `crit-1` by itself.
