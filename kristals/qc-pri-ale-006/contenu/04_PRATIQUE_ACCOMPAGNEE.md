# 04 — Guided practice with fading hints

Three progressively less supported tasks. The learner reads the exact English text; assistance is progressively removed. Teacher may read aloud the text or transcribe a spoken answer, but must record assistance.

## G1 — Identify evidence (strong guidance)

> Nia carefully wrapped a glass jar in a towel before putting it into her backpack. “Please keep this one upright,” she told her friend.

**Step A — Highlighted clues are supplied:** “carefully wrapped,” “glass jar,” “keep this one upright.” **Step B:** Classify “Nia has a backpack” (stated fact) and “Nia wants to prevent damage or spills” (inference). **Step C:** Complete “I infer ... because the text says ...”.

**Worked correction:** Fact — Nia puts a jar into a backpack. Inference — she wants the jar to remain safe or avoid spilling; fragile glass, towel and instruction to keep upright are clues. *Do not invent what is inside the jar.*

**Hint:** “Which words show extra care?” If the student says “Nia is frightened,” ask what phrase could establish fear; none does.

## G2 — Build one explanation (medium guidance)

> After the last rehearsal, Mateo read the list on the door. His name was next to “stage manager.” He wrote down the times for the next three practices and asked where the spare batteries were stored.

**Task:** Infer something about Mateo's **new responsibility**. Quote or paraphrase two clues and complete “I infer ... because ...”. The teacher supplies only the sentence frame, **not** highlighted clues.

**Possible correction:** “Mateo may need to organize parts of the next performance, because his name is next to ‘stage manager,’ and he notes rehearsal times and asks about equipment.” Accept cautious readings of responsibility; reject “He wants to become an actor” as unsupported.

**Feedback if shallow:** “A job title is stated. What unstated task or responsibility can we reasonably expect from the actions?”

## G3 — Two independent ideas (minimal guidance)

> The notice said the outdoor games would begin at ten. At 9:45, volunteers carried a table under the covered porch. One volunteer covered the boxes with a plastic sheet. People arriving at the park waited near the porch instead of walking onto the field.

**Task:** Write **two different** inferences, each with at least one relevant clue and an explanation. There are **no preselected clues** or sentence starters.

**Example acceptable response A:** “The volunteers may be concerned about the weather, because they moved a table under cover and placed a plastic sheet on boxes.”

**Example acceptable response B:** “The games might be delayed or waiting for a decision, because it is almost ten and arriving participants are staying by the porch rather than going onto the field.” The text does not confirm a delay; the cautious form matters. An equally supported suggestion that people are waiting for instructions is acceptable.

**Typical unacceptable response:** “There is a dangerous storm.” Why not? The text never mentions wind, rain or a weather warning; covering supplies permits a weather precaution, not certainty about a storm.

**Fading rule:** After G3 do not reveal a correct answer before a learner explains a link; ask only “Where does the text show that?” Proceed to independent practice when the learner can build two chains without the teacher selecting the clue.
