# 03 — Explicit teaching and thinking aloud

## Lesson target

*I can infer information the writer does not state directly and justify my idea with words from the passage.* (`obj-1`, `crit-1`.) Display **Text says → I infer → because**. Project the same transcript available on paper. Read it aloud without altering details.

## Teaching text: “Bus Stop” (original fiction)

> The bus was turning the corner when Ellis reached the stop. Ellis lifted an arm, took two quick steps toward the curb, and kept watching the driver. The bus slowed down. Ellis lowered the arm and stepped back behind the yellow line.

### Teacher models every step

1. **What is stated?** “The bus was turning the corner”; Ellis “lifted an arm”; the bus “slowed down”; Ellis stepped “behind the yellow line.” These are observations.
2. **Choose an unstated idea.** “Ellis may want the driver to stop so Ellis can board.” The writer does **not** say “Ellis wants to take the bus.”
3. **Underline evidence.** “Lifted an arm,” “kept watching the driver,” “the bus slowed down.” The motion is aimed at the driver; the slowdown is consistent with a signal.
4. **Explain the bridge.** *“I infer that Ellis is trying to catch the bus because Ellis raises an arm toward the driver just as the bus approaches.”*
5. **Add a second inference.** “Ellis may be trying to remain safe while waiting.” Evidence: “stepped back behind the yellow line” after the bus slowed. Connection: remaining behind the line keeps a buffer from the vehicle. We do **not** claim Ellis “always” obeys every safety rule.
6. **Check for overreach.** “Ellis is going to a sports practice” is not supported. We cannot tell the destination.

### Ask the learner to distinguish

- *“The bus slowed down.”* → **stated fact**.
- *“Ellis hopes the bus will stop.”* → **inference**, supported by signalling and watching the driver.
- *“Ellis misses the bus every day.”* → **unsupported invention**.

Model a cautious alternative: Ellis could be signalling someone through the window, but the details about watching the driver and the bus slowing lend support to a boarding intention. The point is to justify, not to claim certainty.

## We practise together with a short new passage

> Amina placed two spare umbrellas beside the front door. Dark clouds were moving over the houses. A few minutes later, her cousins arrived without coats.

**Prompt:** “What might Amina expect?”  
**Possible answer:** “She may be preparing for rain, because dark clouds are approaching and she puts spare umbrellas by the door.”  
**Not warranted:** “The cousins will definitely get soaked.” The text does not say rain occurs.

Ask for one clue first, then the “because” explanation. On a board, draw arrows from “dark clouds” and “spare umbrellas” to the inference. Then remove the arrows and invite the learner to supply a new inference independently.

## Exit check (2 minutes)

Give the sentence “The children waited by the door with their coats on.” Ask: *“What is definitely stated? What may be inferred? What else would you need to know before saying why?”*  
**Expected:** Stated — they wear coats and wait by door. Possible inference — they may be preparing to leave, but purpose/destination unknown. One clue — coats on and waiting by door; both are observable.

This exit check guides the next practice; it is not an independent mastery proof.
