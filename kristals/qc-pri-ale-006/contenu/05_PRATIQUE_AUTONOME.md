# 05 — Four independent reading practices (P1–P4)

Work without hints about which words to select. Read the passage first; then mark evidence and write your inference in English. A verbal response may be recorded or transcribed verbatim. **These passages are different from those reserved for final proofs `q-011` and `q-012`.** Responses below are exemplars, not exact phrases that must be repeated.

## P1 — A book return (narrative, one inference)

> The last page was missing from the library book. As Ada checked the pages again, she placed a small note inside the cover. She walked to the library counter and asked whether there was another copy of the book.

**Task:** What might Ada want to do, and what clues suggest it?

**Example answer:** “Ada may want to finish reading the story, because she checks for the missing last page and asks for another copy.” Do not assert that Ada damaged the book: nothing says so.

**Criteria:** plausible unstated goal; two identifiable actions; explicit causal link; no invented blame.

## P2 — The rehearsal sign (informational, one inference)

> **Choir rehearsal:** The main door will be locked after 5:00 p.m. Anyone arriving later should use the side entrance beside the auditorium. A volunteer will be there until 5:15 p.m.

**Task:** Why might the organizer give detailed directions for late arrivals? Justify.

**Example answer:** “The organizer may want late singers to find the group without being locked out, because the main door locks at five and the side entrance is staffed for fifteen more minutes.” “Everyone is late” is not supported.

**Criteria:** unstated purpose, door/time clue, sound explanation, uncertainty.

## P3 — A repaired model (narrative, two separate inferences)

> On the day of the science fair, Priya carried her bridge model with both hands. One side had fresh tape across a crack. She stopped at a table, pressed the tape down once more, and then placed the model on display. When visitors approached, she pointed to the taped corner before answering questions.

**Task:** Infer **two different** ideas about Priya's actions. For each: identify exact words and explain.

**Example A:** “Priya may want to keep the model stable, because she carries it with both hands and presses down the tape over a crack.”

**Example B:** “Priya may plan to explain the repair to visitors, because she points at the taped corner before answering questions.”

**Limits:** It is not stated who caused the crack; avoid assigning blame. Two versions of “she is being careful” count as only one inference.

## P4 — Water-station schedule (informational, two separate inferences)

> **Fun run water station:** Volunteers should bring full bottles to Table A by 8:30 a.m. A second team will carry empty bottles to the collection bin after the runners pass. The collection bin is behind the finish line, away from the walking route.

**Task:** Make two different inferences about the organizers’ intentions. Cite words for each.

**Example A:** “They likely want water available before runners arrive, because volunteers must bring full bottles by 8:30.”

**Example B:** “They may want to keep the route clear and collect waste safely, because the bin is behind the finish line and away from the walking route.”

**Limits:** Do not claim the water station has a particular number of participants; that figure is absent.

## Self-check before submitting

- Did I write an **unstated** idea rather than copy a fact?
- Which specific phrase in the text supports **each** idea?
- Did I explain *how* the phrase supports it rather than append an unrelated quotation?
- Can I avoid claiming certainty where more than one explanation fits?
- Are my two inferences genuinely different when the task requests two?

**Teacher recording:** preserve responses without turning the sample explanations into a fill-in-the-blank assessment. Feedback should ask for repair, then use another unseen passage.
