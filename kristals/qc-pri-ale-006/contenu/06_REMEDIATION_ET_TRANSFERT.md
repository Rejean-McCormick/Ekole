# 06 — Targeted remediation, transfer and spaced retrieval

## Feedback → small repair → new attempt

| Observed difficulty | Specific response from instructor | Short repair and evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Copies an explicit sentence as an inference | “That line is stated; what does it help you work out?” | Present “A bag stood by the door. A coat lay on it.” The bag's location is a fact; *someone may be preparing to leave* is possible if linked to bag/coat. |
| Guesses without a clue | “Find exact text that makes your idea reasonable.” | Provide one-sentence passage “Rae checked the forecast and put boots by the door.” Support *preparing for wet weather* only if student connects forecast and boots; do not infer a storm is certain. |
| Gives a clue with no reasoning | “Explain what the detail suggests, using ‘because’.” | Rebuild **text → idea → bridge** orally; then remove the frame on another short passage. |
| Claims certainty about feelings or identity | “Can another interpretation fit? What is not said?” | Replace “must be angry” with “may be upset,” only when additional text supports it; reject identity-based assumptions. |
| Produces two paraphrases of the same inference | “Find a second claim about another action or purpose.” | Require two distinct ideas, each with at least one new supporting textual detail. |

## T1 — Transfer: event organization (public announcement, not narrative)

> **Workshop update:** The community centre moved its map-making session from Room 3 to the hall beside the front entrance. Room 3 holds twelve people; the hall has larger tables and more space to spread out paper. Staff will put direction signs near the old room. The start time remains 1 p.m.

**Task:** Give two reasonable inferences about **why** the organizers made changes, with a clue and a why-link for each.

**Exemplar 1:** “They may want more working space, because the hall has larger tables and room to spread out paper, unlike Room 3.”
**Exemplar 2:** “They likely want participants to find the new room, because they will place direction signs near the old room.”
**Limits:** The text does not tell us the number of registrants or whether someone complained.

## T2 — Transfer: fictional field-note observations (informational record)

> **Classroom seed trial, Day 5:** Tray A stood near a sunny window. Tray B was behind the bookcase. Both trays received the same amount of water. On Day 5, five seedlings in Tray A had green leaves. Two seedlings in Tray B were visible, and both leaves looked pale. The class decided to move Tray B to a brighter shelf before the next observation.

**Task:** Formulate two *cautious* inferences about the team's interpretation of the observations; distinguish from conclusions the data cannot prove.

**Exemplar 1:** “The class may think light affects the seedlings, because the sunny tray has more green seedlings than the shaded tray and the group moves Tray B to brighter light.”
**Exemplar 2:** “They may want to test whether brighter placement changes Tray B's growth, because the next step is to move it before observing again.”
**Limits:** This miniature fictional record cannot prove sunlight alone caused the difference; no replication, soil equivalence or temperature controls are given. The evaluated skill is inference from the *English text*, **not** experimental certification.

## Delayed recall (not the final mastery proof)

- **About 2–3 days later:** without showing the original examples, ask for the difference between *stated*, *inferred* and *unsupported*, then give: “Jules put a raincoat in a bag after hearing the forecast.” Student proposes a cautious inference, identifies the forecast/raincoat clue and bridges the ideas. Accept *anticipates rain*; don't claim rain must occur.
- **About 1–2 weeks later:** provide an unfamiliar three-sentence school announcement; ask for two separate inferences and exact support. Record whether the student needs hints; choose an actual unseen text before administration and add it to an instructor-controlled bank after human review.
- **After failure:** reteach **only** the deficient link using a different mini-passage, then reassess independently; never award mastery from a repeated sample answer.

## Accessibility / risk controls

Offer the exact text in audio and text, line spacing, generous time, oral capture or transcription, and neutral names and topics. No background knowledge of specific Quebec institutions is necessary. Do not score pronunciation, handwriting or speed in a reading-inference task. Support accessibility without identifying the inference or selecting the clue for the student during a mastery proof.
