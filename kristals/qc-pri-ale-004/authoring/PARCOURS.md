# Microparcours — `qc-pri-ale-004`

**Discipline (Québec):** Anglais, langue d’enseignement  
**Course language:** English for all learner-facing instruction and tasks; source YAML language metadata preserved unchanged.  
**Status:** v1.2 authored candidate, human pedagogical review pending.

## 1. Intention et frontières
- `obj-1` — **Immutable goal:** Reconstituer actions, personnages et idées d’un récit en anglais.
- `crit-1` — **Immutable mastery evidence:** Résumer une histoire et citer un détail qui soutient le résumé.
- **Learner-facing target (English):** “I can tell who a story is about, explain the important events in order, summarize what changed, and point to a detail in the text that supports my summary.”
- **Scope:** narrative comprehension using *plot, character, event, evidence*. Distinguish an event from a minor detail and a text-based claim from a guess. Do not score handwriting, speed, personal background knowledge or agreement with a character's choices.
- **Out of scope:** formal literary criticism, advanced symbolism, external author biographies or an official PFEQ equivalence. The course does not assume a fixed year or cycle.
- **Texts and provenance:** all named short stories in `contenu/` and assessment items are new fictional texts created for this Kristal; they make no claims about real people or historical events.
## 2. Notions et représentations
- **Plot:** the connected sequence of important story events, including a beginning situation, a challenge or change, attempts or responses, and an outcome. A plot is *not* the same as a list of every sentence.
- **Character:** a person, animal or imagined being who takes part in a story. A setting is not a character. Ask: “Who acts or experiences the problem?”
- **Event:** something that happens or changes during the story, such as a decision or discovery. Separate the main event from small descriptive details.
- **Evidence:** an exact word, phrase, sentence, or unmistakably identified action *present in the text* that supports a claim. Quote precisely or paraphrase faithfully and state the relevant part of the text.
- **Representations:** story map (character → challenge → action → outcome), 3-step timeline (first/next/finally), oral retell, print or accessible text, and evidence chart (claim | matching detail). Equivalent speech-to-text or scribed responses preserve the reading-comprehension criteria.
- **Teaching anchor:** `contenu/01_NOTIONS_ET_EXEMPLES.md`: the lantern story is solved with a model story map; the paper bridge is a second worked example. Counterexamples reveal unsupported inference and inaccurate sequence.
## 3. Préparation / diagnostic
- **Strict prerequisite:** `qc-pri-ale-003` — Lire avec fluidité en anglais. A short recorded or live reading check can attest an equivalent skill and prevent a needless lock; do not silently delete the graph edge.
- **No suggested blocking order** and no reinforcement edges for this leaf.
- `diag-1`, two tasks: listen/read a brief story and identify character/event; read the same kind of text independently and quote a supporting detail. See `contenu/02_DIAGNOSTIC.md` for original stimuli, correct responses and decision rules.
- **Interpretation:** if oral understanding is strong but independent decoding prevents access, target fluent reading and provide a temporary read-aloud in teaching. If the learner can accurately independently read and justify a story detail, document equivalent prerequisite acquisition. A listening-only success must not be taken as sufficient evidence of independent reading fluency.
- **Preparation:** present the learner target and a 4-square story map; do not reveal a future mastery text.
## 4. Enseignement guidé
- `teach-1` — Read **The Lantern Path** once for meaning, a second time to find evidence. Think aloud: “Nia wants the path to be visible. The first arrangement fails at the last turn. She moves two lanterns, so the path becomes easier to follow.”
- Fill the map: character = Nia; problem = dark path and poorly spaced lanterns; response = moving two lanterns closer; outcome = children find the gate. Cite “the last turn was still dark” and “Nia moved two lanterns closer to the corner.”
- Summarize without copying all sentences: “Nia improves a dark garden path by changing the lantern positions so younger students can find the gate.” Support it with the second quoted detail.
- Compare **The Paper Bridge**: “The bridge bent” shows a problem; “Juno folded the sides” supports the solution. The fact that the car was a toy is secondary to the plot.
- Ask learners to explain why “Nia built a new garden” is unsupported; point back to the exact words, not to plausibility.
- Materials and detailed teacher script: `contenu/03_ENSEIGNEMENT_GUIDE.md`.
## 5. Pratique accompagnée
- `guided-1` consists of three fully scripted exercises with fading prompts: **The Paper Bridge** (story-map cue + cited evidence), **The Missing Recipe** (timeline only), **The Quiet Signal** (independent first, one conditional prompt if needed).
- Stage A: prompt with “Who/what went wrong/what changed?”; Stage B: provide a three-box timeline without named characters; Stage C: provide only “Summarize and cite one detail.”
- Feedback model: identify the unsupported or missing claim, point to the passage, elicit correction, and follow with a different passage. Do not equate a fluent-sounding answer with accurate comprehension.
- Teacher keys and sample responses: `contenu/04_PRATIQUE_ACCOMPAGNEE.md`.
## 6. Pratique autonome
- `independent-1`: four fresh passages—**The Seed Cart**, **The Blue Kite**, **The Rainy Concert**, **The Missing Label**—each calls for a short accurate English summary and one specific relevant piece of evidence without teacher hints; see `contenu/05_PRATIQUE_AUTONOME.md`.
- Require character, challenge/important change, response or outcome in logical order; cite a detail that *proves one claim* in the summary. Responses can be spoken, typed or scribed verbatim with no interpretation by the scribe.
- Use source-based scoring, not “one magic sentence”: valid paraphrases and alternative defensible summaries are accepted. Do not give the learner the supplied exemplars before completion.
- Record only evidence and rubric decisions in the LMS, not learner data in canonical documents. These practices prepare for—but do not substitute for—the two unseen mastery demonstrations.
## 7. Feedback et erreurs typiques
- **Wrong character:** “The school fair made the bridge” → ask “Who built it?” and underline the subject of the action; retell using the actual character.
- **List of details without plot:** “There were paper strips, blocks and a car” → use a problem → response → outcome organizer; then remove the organizer on the next story.
- **Sequence reversed:** “The bridge held and then bent” → mark temporal clues (“at first”, “this time”, “the next day”) and reconstruct a timeline.
- **Unsupported explanation:** “Nia used solar lights because she wanted to save energy” → ask for the exact line; if absent, remove the claim or label it as a possible guess, never as text evidence.
- **Misquotation:** if exact words are altered inside quotation marks, model copying the short phrase accurately; faithful paraphrases must not be placed in quotation marks.
- **Feedback loop:** specify error → locate evidence → rewrite a claim → try a different passage → review again after 2 and 7 days.
## 8. Évaluation formative et preuves
- **Family** `qc-pri-ale-004-ev-01`; `objectif_ref: obj-1`; `critere_ref: crit-1`; native type `réponse_argumentée`.
- 12 fixed controlled items in `evaluation/BANQUE_ITEMS.json`, using 12 complete, original story stimuli. Modes are `fixe` (no uncontrolled substitutions), with distinct tasks in initial, intermediate and transfer bands. The last two items are complete uncoached performances: **The Roof Garden** and **The Rolling Library**.
- Rubric: character accuracy, main-event causal sequence, faithful main-idea summary, and relevant text evidence; see `evaluation/GRILLE_MAITRISE.md`. Responses may be oral with a verbatim record, dictated, typed or handwritten; no credit for an invented event.
- **Mastery requires both new contexts**, with at least satisfactory level on every core dimension in each, without cues; a single total score never suffices.
- Judge evidence on a source-locked text, not on an LLM-generated open-ended “answer key.” All 12 items remain `a_revoir` until human language and bias review.
## 9. Remédiation / enrichissement
- For decoding difficulty, use an accessible copy and revisit `qc-pri-ale-003` or verify equivalent fluency; do not confuse supported listening performance with independent reading mastery.
- For missing plot, reduce a narrative to three actions and sort strips before removing this scaffold; for evidence difficulty, teach a two-column “claim | words that show it” organizer.
- For mixed chronology, underline time words and use three numbered boxes; for excessive detail, contrast a list with a concise main idea.
- Enrichment: compare how two settings change a character's decisions, but still defend every summary with evidence. Transfer contexts in `contenu/06_REMEDIATION_ET_TRANSFERT.md` are a nature walk and a backstage performance, with full answer exemplars.
- No cross-Kristal dependency is added; no other Kristal is modified.
## 10. Accessibilité
- Present accessible digital text with resizable font, generous spacing, high contrast and screen-reader compatibility; allow chunked paragraphs and extra processing time.
- Provide text and an optional human-read audio version for teaching; where the objective is *reading comprehension*, record separately whether print was independently decoded or narrated. Do not use an audio-only proof to claim the prerequisite of independent reading fluency.
- Allow speech, keyboard, speech-to-text or verbatim scribing for the summary and evidence; none supplies hints, invented content, or text interpretation.
- Avoid culturally specific background assumptions: every inference needed for scoring appears in the passage. Do not require personally revealing anecdotes or judgement of family circumstances.
- Accommodations and any support level must be documented to interpret autonomy fairly; do not silently lower the four cognitive criteria.
## 11. Critères de sortie
1. Reconstruct characters, important events, and central idea accurately from a **new English narrative**, not a memorized exemplar.
2. Summarize the story coherently in English and quote or faithfully identify at least one **specific supporting textual detail** with a clear connection to a summary claim (`crit-1`).
3. Meet “satisfactory” on all four core dimensions on **two distinct, unfamiliar independent contexts**, including the rooftop garden and mobile library stories (`q-011`, `q-012`). Different wording is accepted if textually justified.
4. Explain a reading decision on request without a teacher-supplied answer. Log whether the reading was independent, and verify the prerequisite or equivalent acquisition.
5. Where evidence is weak, select the matching targeted remediator and retest with a different story. Schedule brief delayed recall checks after 2 and 7 days; maintain status “review required” before human qualification.
