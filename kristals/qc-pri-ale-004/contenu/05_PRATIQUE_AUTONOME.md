# 05 — Four independent reading practices

**Student instructions (English):** Read each story yourself. In 2–3 clear sentences, say who the main character is, what important problem or change happens, what the character does and how the story ends. Then quote one short phrase or accurately paraphrase one specific detail *that supports one of your summary claims*. No story map or teacher hints. Oral or typed summaries are acceptable; reading modality is recorded separately.

## Independent A1 — The Seed Cart
**The Seed Cart** (original instructional story, P1)

> Ava was in charge of the class seed cart. She wanted to share seeds with the younger class, but the packets had no labels. Ava drew a small picture of each plant on its packet and wrote the plant's name beneath it. She checked the labels against the classroom chart. The next morning, the younger students chose seeds confidently. Ava learned that a clear label can help someone make a choice.
**Question:** What challenge does Ava solve, and how? Give one relevant detail from the story.

**Example key (withhold from learner):** “Ava wants younger children to choose seeds but discovers that the packets have no labels. She makes and checks picture-and-name labels, so students can choose the seeds.” **Evidence:** “She checked the labels against the classroom chart.” This supports careful checking; “The next morning, the younger students chose seeds confidently” supports the positive result. Do **not** reward “she grew every seed” (unwritten).

## Independent A2 — The Blue Kite
**The Blue Kite** (original instructional story, P2)

> Eli made a blue kite for the neighborhood festival. At the park, the kite dipped to the ground whenever he tried to fly it. Eli noticed that its tail was very short. He tied two ribbons to the tail and tried again. The kite rose steadily above the grass. Eli ran to show his sister, who was waiting near the trees.
**Question:** Explain how Eli responds when the kite fails. Cite one detail supporting the solution.

**Example key:** “Eli's kite falls when he tries to fly it. He adds two ribbons to its short tail, and the kite then flies steadily.” **Evidence:** “He tied two ribbons to the tail and tried again.” Do not assert that Eli changed the kite's material (not in passage).

## Independent A3 — The Rainy Concert
**The Rainy Concert** (original instructional story, P3)

> The class had planned an outdoor music concert for Friday. In the morning, heavy rain covered the playground. Sora did not want the concert to be cancelled. She helped the teacher move the chairs into the library and arranged a space for the musicians. By noon, the class could perform indoors. Their families listened from the library seats.
**Question:** Summarize how the concert plan changes and use evidence for the outcome.

**Example key:** “Heavy rain prevents the class from holding the concert outside. Sora helps move it into the library, where the class performs for their families.” **Evidence:** “By noon, the class could perform indoors.” Do not say the concert was cancelled.

## Independent A4 — The Missing Label
**The Missing Label** (original instructional story, P4)

> At the community museum, Ben saw a photograph with no information beside it. Visitors could not tell when it had been taken. Ben asked the curator for help. Together they checked an old catalogue and discovered the date and the photographer's name. Ben wrote a new label and placed it beside the photograph. The next visitors could understand what they were seeing.
**Question:** Summarize Ben's problem-solving sequence. What detail supports that the photograph became easier to understand?

**Example key:** “Ben notices that a museum photo has no explanation. He and the curator find its date and photographer in an old catalogue, and Ben adds a label.” **Evidence:** “The next visitors could understand what they were seeing.” Do not claim Ben took the photograph.
