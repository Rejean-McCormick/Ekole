# 06 — Error-specific support, transfers, and spaced retrieval

## Repair one error at a time

| Observable difficulty | Likely confusion | Short response and independent recheck |
|---|---|---|
| Says the setting is the character | Character versus place | Circle verbs and ask “Who performed the action?” Then remove circling and try a fresh paragraph. |
| Lists objects without plot | Details versus main events | Arrange three event strips (problem, response, outcome); write a connected 1-sentence summary; later drop strips. |
| Reverses event order | Timeline and transition words | Mark “at first”, “after”, “finally”; sort 3 events and retell without the organizer. |
| Invents a motive or outcome | Plausible inference treated as a fact | Put “MY CLAIM” beside “TEXT WORDS”; remove a claim if no line supports it. |
| Quotes irrelevant words | Evidence not linked to summary | Ask “What *specific sentence* in your summary does this phrase prove?” Select a more relevant detail. |
| Copies the passage word for word | Cannot condense main idea | Limit summary to challenge + response + result, then verify that it stays truthful. |
| Reads words inaccurately | Prerequisite may be unmet | Strengthen `qc-pri-ale-003` or assess equivalent reading fluency; supported listening comprehension does not prove independent print access. |

## Transfer T1 — Outdoor route (different narrative setting)
**The Trail Markers** (original instructional story, T1)

> On a nature walk, Priya and her group followed signs to a small pond. A strong wind knocked one sign into the grass. Two students turned toward the hill instead. Priya remembered the map printed on their guide sheet. She compared the map with a large fallen tree nearby and found the correct trail. She placed the fallen sign beside the path and told the leader. The group reached the pond together.
**Task:** Give an English summary of Priya's decisions in 2–3 sentences; identify a specific passage detail that supports how she finds the path. **Key:** “When a trail sign falls, two students head the wrong way. Priya uses her guide map and a fallen tree to identify the right trail, then alerts the leader and the group reaches the pond.” **Evidence:** “She compared the map with a large fallen tree nearby and found the correct trail.” **Typical error:** “Priya replaced every sign”; not in the text. **Transfer emphasis:** evidence can describe *how* an action works, not just that it happened.

## Transfer T2 — Community theater (another distinct setting)
**The Stage Curtain** (original instructional story, T2)

> During a community play, the curtain jammed just before the second scene. Malik, who was helping backstage, saw that a loose ribbon had caught in the track. He told the stage manager instead of pulling the curtain. The manager stopped the show for a moment and safely removed the ribbon. The actors returned, and the audience watched the rest of the play.
**Task:** Summarize what Malik does when the curtain stops working, and identify evidence that his response leads to a safe resolution. **Key:** “During a play, a ribbon jams the curtain. Malik tells the stage manager, who pauses the show and removes the ribbon, so the play continues.” **Evidence:** “The manager stopped the show for a moment and safely removed the ribbon.” **Typical error:** “Malik pulled the curtain until it opened”; this contradicts “instead of pulling the curtain.” **Transfer emphasis:** distinguish a character's intention from the exact action taken.

## Two short delayed-recall checks

- **After ~2 days:** Without rereading the lantern story, name plot, character, event and evidence, and explain why “Nia built a new gate” was unsupported. Then read a *fresh* micro-story before claiming current competence.
- **After ~7 days:** With a new short fictional passage, produce a 2-sentence summary and one linked detail independently. Record whether the learner transfers the process; an accurate memory of the old story is not a substitute for a new reading.

## Enrichment

Compare characters' choices across T1 and T2: Priya checks a map; Malik notifies a manager. Both respond to a problem, but their actions differ. Cite one exact detail for *each* and avoid ranking the people without text evidence. Extension success is not required for baseline mastery.
