# 01 — Understanding narrative texts: concepts and worked examples

**Learning target:** “I can identify who acts, what happens and why it matters, then support my summary with details in the text.” **Focus:** plot, character, event, evidence. Read the passage, not personal guesses. An evidence detail must *support* the claim you make; a copied but irrelevant sentence is not enough.

## Four precise words

| Word | Meaning | Important distinction | Check |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Character** | A person, animal or imaginary figure that acts, speaks or experiences events. | “The garden” is a *setting*, not an acting character. | Who makes a decision or faces the problem? |
| **Event** | Something that happens or changes in the story. | A blue scarf may describe a person but does not necessarily change the story. | What happened first, next and last? |
| **Plot** | The connected sequence of significant events: situation, challenge/change, response and outcome. | Plot is not an unrelated list of details or every sentence copied. | What problem or change connects the key events? |
| **Evidence** | Words or an unambiguously identified action in the source that actually backs up a claim about the story. | A plausible guess about what *might* happen is not evidence. | Which exact detail supports this summary claim? |

A **summary** is a short, accurate reconstruction of the central character(s), key challenge, response and outcome in connected sentences. It omits incidental details without changing events. A **quotation** uses exactly the source's words inside quotation marks; a **paraphrase** faithfully restates the source in new words without quotation marks.

## Worked example 1 — Dark garden path
**The Lantern Path** (original instructional story, M0)

> After school, Nia noticed that the path to the garden was dark. She wanted younger students to reach the garden safely. She asked the caretaker for permission to hang battery-powered lanterns along the fence. At first, the lanterns were too far apart, and the last turn was still dark. Nia moved two lanterns closer to the corner. The next evening, the whole path was easy to see. Nia smiled when the younger students found the gate without stopping.
1. **Character:** Nia; younger students are affected but Nia makes the main decisions.
2. **Beginning / challenge:** the path is dark; the first lantern spacing leaves the last turn dark.
3. **Action / outcome:** Nia moves two lanterns; later students find the gate easily.
4. **Accurate summary:** “Nia makes the garden path easier for younger students to see by repositioning lanterns after her first plan fails.”
5. **Evidence with connection:** The words “Nia moved two lanterns closer to the corner” support the claim that she revised the lantern positions. The line “the last turn was still dark” supports *why* a revision was needed.
6. **What to omit:** the caretaker's job title is not needed for this short main-idea summary.

## Worked example 2 — A bridge that bends
**The Paper Bridge** (original instructional story, G1)

> At the school fair, Juno built a paper bridge for a toy car. The bridge bent when the car reached the middle. Juno folded the sides of a new strip of paper and placed two blocks beneath its ends. This time the bridge held the car. Juno showed her classmates how the folds made the bridge stronger.
- **Story map:** Juno → a paper bridge bends → she folds a new strip and uses blocks → the bridge holds the car.
- **Accurate summary:** “Juno repairs her paper-bridge design by folding the sides and supporting the ends so that it can hold a toy car.”
- **Exact evidence:** “This time the bridge held the car.” This supports the successful outcome; “Juno folded the sides of a new strip of paper” supports the strategy.
- **Idea:** testing and redesigning a model improves it. This is an interpretation of the events, *not* a claim that Juno invented a new type of paper.

## Two counterexamples (do not copy these as correct answers)

1. **Incorrect:** “Nia built a new gate so students could enter the garden.” **Why wrong:** the passage says she moved lanterns; it says nothing about building a gate. **Repair:** “Nia moved two lanterns closer to the corner so the path became visible.”
2. **Incorrect:** “Juno's bridge held the car, then bent and broke.” **Why wrong:** the order is reversed, and “broke” was never stated. **Repair:** “The first bridge bent; after Juno changed its design, the bridge held the car.”

## Quick comprehension check

- **Question:** Is “The paper bridge bent” a character, event or setting? **Key:** event; the bridge changes shape.
- **Question:** Does “Juno loved physics more than art” count as evidence? **Key:** no; this is not in the text.
- **Question:** What makes an evidence detail useful? **Key:** it must come from the passage *and directly support a particular summary statement*.

**Teacher note:** Invite multiple truthful phrasings, but insist on correct character, coherent event sequence, main idea and a specific linked detail. No story assumes information outside the passage.
