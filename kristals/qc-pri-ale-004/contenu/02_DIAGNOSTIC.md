# 02 — Short diagnostic: reading access and narrative meaning

**Purpose:** distinguish prerequisite reading fluency from narrative-comprehension needs. No grade, label or permanent lock is decided from a single attempt. Read tasks and give feedback separately. Do not reveal mastery passages.

## Diagnostic task D1 — Listening/reading comprehension

**Stimulus (original):** “Lena put her drawing by the window. A breeze blew the paper onto the floor. Lena closed the window and clipped the drawing to a board. The drawing stayed in place.”

**Administration:** The teacher reads the four sentences aloud *once or twice*; the learner can also see the text. Ask: (a) Who acts? (b) What happens to the drawing? (c) What does Lena do? (d) Which exact words show the result? **Model responses:** (a) Lena; (b) the breeze blows it onto the floor; (c) she closes the window and clips it to a board; (d) “The drawing stayed in place.” **Diagnostic interpretation:** three correct plot details and a source-specific result indicate comprehension with a listening scaffold, **not proof of independent reading fluency**. Misidentifying the breeze as the character invites a character/action organizer.

## Diagnostic task D2 — Independent English reading

**Stimulus (original):** “At the market, Jay forgot his shopping list. He could not remember which vegetables the cook needed. He called the cook and wrote down the names on a small card. Jay bought the right vegetables and returned to the kitchen.”

**Administration:** Learner reads the written passage independently (no audio prompt) and explains: (a) Why does Jay call? (b) Summarize the solution in one sentence. (c) Give a detail that shows it worked. Optionally observe word reading and phrasing unobtrusively; no timed-speed cutoff is assumed.

**Key:** (a) He forgot the list and cannot remember the requested vegetables; (b) “Jay calls the cook, writes down the names and buys the needed vegetables”; (c) “Jay bought the right vegetables and returned to the kitchen.” An equivalent faithful paraphrase passes. **Incorrect but plausible:** “Jay asks another shopper to choose the vegetables” — not in the story. **Reading note:** document if independent decoding was labored enough to prevent access; do not infer a disability.

## Decision guide

- **Both accessible and accurate:** proceed to the narrative lesson; document equivalent readiness even if `qc-pri-ale-003` was not previously visited.
- **D1 accurate, D2 decoding barrier:** teach narrative meaning with accessible text while strengthening or diagnosing independent reading through `qc-pri-ale-003`. Do not mark independent-read prerequisite as met solely by D1.
- **D1/D2 readable, but causality or evidence missing:** start with the story map and claim/evidence chart (03) without requiring a full return to the prior Kristal.
- **Mixed evidence:** brief targeted support, then a *different* four-sentence passage; no blanket failure based on a single score.

**Accessibility:** larger text, high contrast, time, keyboard or oral answers allowed; record whether teacher read the narrative aloud. Avoid changing the language demands unnoticed when comparing tasks.
