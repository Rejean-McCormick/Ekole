# 04 — Three guided exercises: prompts fade away

## Guided G1 — Paper bridge (full scaffold)
**The Paper Bridge** (original instructional story, G1)

> At the school fair, Juno built a paper bridge for a toy car. The bridge bent when the car reached the middle. Juno folded the sides of a new strip of paper and placed two blocks beneath its ends. This time the bridge held the car. Juno showed her classmates how the folds made the bridge stronger.
**Learner tasks:** (1) Who acts? (2) What goes wrong? (3) What changes? (4) Tell the plot in 1–2 sentences. (5) Copy one detail that supports your explanation.

**Help level 1:** Show the frame: “At first ___, so Juno ___. After that ___.” **Help level 2 (only if needed):** Point to “The bridge bent” and ask which later sentence shows success.

**Key:** Juno / bridge bends / she folds a new strip and adds supports / next test works. **Possible summary:** “Juno changes her paper bridge after it bends, and her improved bridge holds the toy car.” **Evidence:** “This time the bridge held the car.” **Feedback if character/setting confused:** “The fair is the place; Juno is the person acting.” **Fade rule:** remove the filled frame on G2.

## Guided G2 — Missing recipe (partial scaffold)
**The Missing Recipe** (original instructional story, G2)

> Omar promised to make soup for the class picnic. On Saturday, he opened the recipe book, but the page he needed was missing. Omar remembered that his grandmother had written the recipe on a card. He found the card inside a kitchen drawer and followed the steps. On Sunday, he brought warm soup to the picnic, and everyone had a bowl.
**Learner tasks:** Fill an *empty* FIRST → NEXT → LAST timeline, then give a 1–2 sentence English summary with one proof detail. No character or event labels are supplied.

**Key timeline:** page missing → Omar remembers and finds grandmother's card → he follows it and serves soup at picnic. **Possible summary:** “When a recipe page is missing, Omar finds an old recipe card and uses it to make soup for the picnic.” **Evidence:** “He found the card inside a kitchen drawer and followed the steps.” **Feedback:** If the answer says Omar bought soup, ask “Can you point to the sentence?” and revise to making soup. **Fade rule:** remove the timeline on G3; only provide a question if the learner stalls.

## Guided G3 — Quiet signal (minimal scaffold)
**The Quiet Signal** (original instructional story, G3)

> When the school orchestra rehearsed, Mei could not hear the conductor's final instruction. Some players stopped while others continued. Mei suggested that the conductor raise a bright blue card to signal the ending. During the next rehearsal, everyone saw the card and stopped together. The conductor kept the card for future rehearsals.
**Learner task (initially no prompts):** Summarize Mei's story in two English sentences and provide one detail that shows how her idea worked. If the learner struggles, give **one** conditional hint: “Find the problem, then find what Mei suggests.” Do not supply the answer.

**Exemplar:** “At rehearsal, the players stop at different times because they cannot all hear the final direction. Mei suggests a blue card that everyone can see, and the orchestra stops together.” **Evidence:** “During the next rehearsal, everyone saw the card and stopped together.”

**Feedback if wrong:** “The blue card is not a character; Mei is. The card is an action tool.” If evidence is only “a card,” request the *sentence showing it worked*. **Fade rule:** G3 ends only once learner can generate a supported summary without the frame; the hint, if used, is documented and not counted as mastery.

## Teacher observation, repeated on each exercise

Record **character**, **sequence**, **main idea**, **text support** as secure / developing / needs help. Record the *level of hints used*, not just a total mark. For errors, point to a textual line and have learner repair the answer; follow with a new story rather than repeating the same answer. Accept equivalent correct English and accessible response modes.
