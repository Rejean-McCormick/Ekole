# Assessment plan — qc-pri-ale-004 (candidate, not certified)

**Leaf / family:** `qc-pri-ale-004` / `qc-pri-ale-004-ev-01`. **Objective:** `obj-1` — Reconstituer actions, personnages et idées d’un récit en anglais. **Criterion:** `crit-1` — Résumer une histoire et citer un détail qui soutient le résumé. **Native family type:** `réponse_argumentée`. **Bank format:** `qc.learning-item-bank/1.0`.

## Evidence design

- Each of **12 fixed, distinct, original narrative stimuli** is provided *in full* inside its bank item, with its exact character/event key, model English summary, supporting verbatim quotation, evidence connection and a plausible but unsupported claim.
- Item modes are all `fixe`: **no parametric generation and no random seed**. Corpus replacements would require text, key, readability and bias review before versioning. Language models are **not** validators of student correctness.
- Each item maps to `obj-1`, `crit-1`, and `qc-pri-ale-004-ev-01`. Coverage grows from identifying the key character/event, through arranging cause and effect, to supporting a coherent independent summary in a new context.
- Responses must be evaluated by the same four dimensions (character, chronology/causality, central idea, linked evidence). Accept other text-faithful wordings; an answer is *not* wrong for differing from the model phrasing.

- **Initial `q-001..004`**: short source-locked narratives; character, change, order and a detail.
- **Intermediate `q-005..010`**: longer cause-effect narratives, potential misreadings and compact summaries supported by source action.
- **Transfer `q-011` / `q-012`**: **two separate previously unseen contexts**, a *rooftop herb garden* and a *mobile library cart*. Both require a coherent independent summary plus a relevant text detail; a learner cannot substitute the same piece of writing for both.
- Text-first presentation; the story-map representation is a **teaching aid**, not compulsory during mastery. Alternatives: high-contrast/reflowable print, accessible digital text, chunking without answer prompts, extra time, keyboard, speech-to-text or verbatim scribing for output.

## Rubric and decision

1. **Character:** correct central agent(s) and role(s).
2. **Events / plot:** important challenge, response and outcome in a defensible sequence, without false addition.
3. **Summary:** concise, central idea accurately reconstructed in English.
4. **Evidence:** exact or faithfully paraphrased *relevant* text detail, clearly linked to a summary claim.

Score 0–3 in each dimension; levels are anchored in `evaluation/GRILLE_MAITRISE.md`. For mastery, every dimension must reach **at least 2**, *separately on both q-011 and q-012*, independently. A total, average or QCM score alone cannot produce mastery. Intermediate practice is diagnostic rather than a substitute for two different contexts. Difficulties are editorial design labels, not psychometrically calibrated values.

## Scoring boundaries / accessibility / safeguarding

- Variability permitted: defensible summary order when meaning remains correct; faithful synonym choices; relevant alternate text quotations; accessible response modality. No invented material, changed story events or outside assumptions.
- Record whether the learner *read the printed text* independently; teacher oral delivery makes the result supported comprehension, not independent decoding. The reading-fluency prerequisite `qc-pri-ale-003` may be satisfied through a separate diagnostic evidencing equivalent acquisition.
- Do not score accent, voice quality, typing speed, handwriting, spelling mistakes that do not change the meaning, identity, home situation or literary taste. Do not demand private information; all stories are fictional and nonviolent.
- No copyrighted extracts, generated images or audio files are included. Confirm accessibility in the target LMS (screen-reader ordering and font resizing) before use.

## Delivery and human review

The bank keys and textual quotations are checked by script for internal source consistency and two distinct mastery contexts. The items, scoring examples, text difficulty, linguistic correctness, possible ambiguity/bias and suitability for learners require a **human English-language and pedagogy review**, followed by piloting; no external validation or certification is asserted.
