# 01 — Word parts and context: notions and solved examples

**Goal (`obj-1`):** Infer a new English word's meaning using **both** morphology and its sentence. **Evidence (`crit-1`):** Explain the meanings of new words in two sentences. Reading meaning, not memorizing affix names, is the aim.

## Four essential notions

| Term | Exact working meaning | A transparent model | What not to assume |
|---|---|---|---|
| **prefix** | A meaningful piece placed *before* a base/root | `re-` + `write` = **rewrite**, write again | The letters `un` at the start of *uncle* are **not** automatically a prefix. |
| **suffix** | A meaningful piece attached *after* a base/root | `care` + `-ful` = **careful**, showing care | End letters are not necessarily a suffix; a suffix's function varies by word. |
| **root** | The core meaning-bearing element of a word; here we begin with easily recognizable bases | `help` in **helpful**, **helpless**, **unhelpful** | Some real roots cannot stand alone; our easy examples are not a universal parsing rule. |
| **context** | Words and situation around a target that help select its likely sense | A jar is used **again** → *reuse* likely means *use again* | Word parts make a *hypothesis*, not a proof of meaning without checking the sentence. |

**Useful pieces:** `un-` often expresses *not/the opposite of*; `re-` often means *again*; `mis-` often means *wrongly*; `-ful` often means *full of/showing*; `-less` often means *without*; `-er` sometimes means *a person who does an action* (e.g., *teacher*), but not always. The word *invaluable* does not mean worthless: do not use mechanical addition as the only check. Spelling changes may occur (e.g., `happy + -ness → happiness`).

## Four-step reading routine

1. **Read** the entire English sentence and underline a meaningful clue (action, result, contrast, or example).
2. **Spot** a plausible root and prefix or suffix; box only genuine parts.
3. **Infer** a short meaning in this sentence. Decide if your guess fits the clue.
4. **Explain:** “I think **[word]** means **[...]** because **[part]** suggests **[...]**, and **[phrase from the sentence]** shows **[...]**.”

**Screen-reader version:** state target, root, prefix/suffix, quoted context clue, inferred meaning in that order. Color is never essential.

## Solved example 1 — A library note (prefix)

**Sentence A:** “The first draft was hard to follow, so Nora decided to **rewrite** the opening paragraph before sharing it.”

- Part: `re-` (*again*) + `write` (root: compose words).
- Context: “first draft was hard to follow” and “before sharing it” suggest a second attempt.
- **Reasoned meaning:** *rewrite* means to write the paragraph again in a revised way, not simply to read it aloud.
- Proof statement: “*Rewrite* means write again or revise. The prefix *re-* signals repetition; the difficult first draft explains why Nora is doing it again.”

## Solved example 2 — A craft project (suffix)

**Sentence B:** “Ben was **careful** with the glass bowl; he carried it slowly using both hands so it would not break.”

- Part: root `care` + `-ful`, expressing a quality of showing care.
- Context: “slowly using both hands” and “so it would not break” show attention and caution.
- **Reasoned meaning:** *careful* means acting with care to avoid damage or mistakes, not afraid of bowls.
- Proof statement: “*Careful* describes someone who acts with care; *care + -ful* supports it, and his slow movement with both hands confirms it.”

## Counterexamples with corrections

1. **False split:** “*Uncle* starts with `un-`, so it must mean *not a cle*.” **Correction:** no relevant standalone `cle` root supports that split; *uncle* is a family noun. Check a meaningful base and the sentence before claiming an affix.
2. **Blind prefix guess:** “*Rewrite* means *write the opposite* because it has a prefix.” **Correction:** prefixes have different meanings. `re-` usually indicates repetition, and the library sentence demands a new draft.
3. **Overliteral meaning:** “*Careful* means *filled with liquid care* because `-ful` always means full of something physical.” **Correction:** `-ful` can mark a quality; actions in the sentence establish *cautious, attentive*.
4. **Unjustified dictionary guess:** “The *wireless* device has **less** technology.” **Correction:** the adjective usually means operating without physical wires; the independent comparative word *less* is different. A sentence about no cable confirms this sense.

## Practice conversation with answer guide

Teacher: “What does the word part suggest?” Learner: “A possible meaning.” Teacher: “Which exact words in the sentence confirm it?” Learner identifies a quote and revises if necessary. Do not confuse a correct lucky guess with explained comprehension.
