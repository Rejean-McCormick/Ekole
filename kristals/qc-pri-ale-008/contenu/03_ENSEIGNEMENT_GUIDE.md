# 03 — Explicit teaching and modelled explanations

**Preparation:** a projected English sentence, a pencil/highlighter (or plain-text labels), and a two-column card: **Word parts / Sentence clues**. All examples here are original.

## 1. State the goal (1 minute)

“Today we will infer what an unfamiliar word means, not by guessing from one small piece, but by testing our guess against what the whole sentence says. You will explain **two different words from two English sentences**.”

## 2. Think aloud: prefix + context (5 minutes)

**Sentence 1:** “The message had several errors, so Ellis will **rewrite** it before sending the final version.”

Teacher model, out loud or in accessible transcript:

1. “I see `re-` before `write`, a base I know. `re-` often means *again*.”
2. “That suggests *write again*; I should check the sentence.”
3. “The words ‘several errors’ and ‘final version’ tell me Ellis is correcting the message.”
4. “I can explain: *rewrite* means write it again in a better or corrected form; `re-` and the errors support this.”

**Check:** Ask, “Which of the two clues comes from a word part? Which comes from the sentence?” Accept ‘re-’ and ‘several errors’/‘final version’ respectively.

## 3. Think aloud: suffix + context (5 minutes)

**Sentence 2:** “Ava held the fragile cup with both hands and walked slowly; she was **careful**.”

Teacher model: root `care`, suffix `-ful` → *showing care*. Context “both hands,” “walked slowly” and “fragile cup” confirms cautious handling. **Explain:** “*Careful* means acting thoughtfully to avoid a mistake or damage; I know this from `care + -ful` and from how Ava carries the cup.”

## 4. Reject a bad hypothesis (3 minutes)

Show: “My **uncle** came to dinner.” Ask whether `un- + cle` is defensible. **Answer:** No; *uncle* is a family word with no productive split here. Contrasting initial letters with meaningful pieces prevents mechanical guessing. Do **not** infer that *un-* is never a real prefix.

## 5. Transfer in teacher-led two-sentence format (3 minutes)

Show the two teaching sentences side by side. Teacher writes two compact responses, each with *meaning → relevant part → quoted sentence phrase*. Learners point to or name each category. Use a printed box if handwriting is a barrier; do not overfocus on spelling.

**Micro-check (not graded):** “Could the sentence evidence change our guess?” Answer yes; it can restrict or correct what an affix suggests. “Can we explain a word simply by saying it ‘has a prefix’?” Answer no; name its meaning and its supporting phrase.

## Teaching cautions

- Never claim that a spelling chunk is necessarily a linguistic morpheme.
- Not all suffixes have one invariant gloss, and actual words may have spelling changes or multiple historical roots.
- Give a model that remains in standard English; accept clear equivalent wording rather than a memorized quotation.
