# 06 — Targeted repair, two transfers and delayed retrieval

## Typical errors → specific feedback and new evidence

| Observed error | Feedback to say | Brief repair (not the scored item) | What counts as progress |
|---|---|---|---|
| Student calls the beginning of any word a prefix | “Find a base that carries meaning. `un-` in *unfair* works differently from letters in *uncle*.” | Sort *unfair, uncle, repaint, rescue* by whether `un-`/`re-` is a transparent prefix (`unfair, repaint` only). | Names real base and prefix in a new word. |
| Student reads `-less` as “a little less” | “The suffix can express absence, while *less* can be a separate comparative word.” | Contrast *wireless mouse* (without wire) with *less water* (smaller amount). | Gives meaning consistent with both suffix and sentence. |
| Student guesses a meaning but ignores sentence | “Point to a few English words that make your idea likely.” | “The **fearless** climber calmly crossed the high bridge despite the crowd’s worries.” Quote “calmly crossed” and connect `fear + -less`. | Cites real evidence, not invented events. |
| Meaning correct but part is incorrect | “Your overall meaning fits. Try the smallest real base and added part.” | Compare `help / helpful / helpless`; do not treat each letter as a morpheme. | Revises segmentation without changing sound meaning. |
| Spelling blocks explanation | “Show your thinking by speaking or dictating; the idea is word meaning.” | Offer accessible answer fields and neutral scribe. | Correct M, P, C independent of spelling mechanics. |

## Transfer T1 — Makerspace instructions (new context)

1. “Before using the printer, pupils must **reconnect** the loose cable so the screen can turn on again.”
2. “The label was **unreadable** because the letters were tiny and the ink had faded.”

**Task:** Explain the target meaning of each word and cite word structure + context. **Reference:** *reconnect* = connect again (`re- + connect`; clues “loose cable”, “turn on again”); *unreadable* = impossible/difficult to read (`un- + read + -able`; clues “letters were tiny”, “ink had faded”). **Evidence:** both independent explanations are supported, not merely guessed.

## Transfer T2 — Nature field note (distinct context)

1. “The **windless** afternoon felt still; not even the tall grass moved.”
2. “The class will **revisit** the pond next month to compare the frogs with what they saw today.”

**Reference:** *windless* = with no wind (`wind + -less`; “not even the tall grass moved”); *revisit* = visit again (`re- + visit`; “next month”, “what they saw today”). **Boundary:** A similar-sounding guess unsupported by both parts is not sufficient.

## Delayed retrieval (2–7 days later; NOT reuse of scored evidence)

Without reviewing notes, answer from two fresh sentences:

1. “The **careless** packer forgot to close the box and several pencils fell out.”
2. “We will **repaint** the bench because the old paint has peeled away.”

**Reference:** *careless* = without sufficient care (`care + -less`, “forgot to close ... pencils fell out”); *repaint* = paint again (`re- + paint`, “old paint has peeled away”). Give feedback after the independent attempt; track whether evidence for both words persists over time. This recall check supplements, never substitutes for, the two final novel contexts.

## Extension and ethical use

Learners ready for more can explore `un + help + ful → unhelpful`: prefix and suffix jointly affect meaning. Ask them to confirm the sense against a context rather than claim any string can be freely constructed. No personal disclosures, special materials or external paid services needed. No automated free-form AI assessor is authorized.
