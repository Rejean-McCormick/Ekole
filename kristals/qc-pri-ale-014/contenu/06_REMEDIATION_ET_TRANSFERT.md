# 06 — Targeted remediation, transfers and delayed recall | `qc-pri-ale-014`

## Diagnostic feedback mapped to a new attempt

| Observable difficulty | Targeted remediation | Fresh verification |
|---|---|---|
| Learner replies to an imaginary earlier question | Listen to a new 2-turn recording or live prompt; identify the question's key request (what / why / how); no grammar lecture first. | Partner asks a *different* why/how question and learner answers relevantly. |
| No space for partner; two speeches in a row | Practice `listen → answer → ask → listen → answer` with temporary turn cards and a timer only if useful; fade cards. | 4 alternating live turns on a new low-stakes topic. |
| Reason repeats claim (“because it is good”) | Compare `because it is nice` to `because it helps students share`, ask which explains *how*. | Support a different fictional project with a concrete benefit. |
| Example restates the reason | Use concrete prompts “Who would do what, and when?” without imposing real names or personal details. | Present another idea with a distinct situational example. |
| Difficulty hearing or speaking in test setting | Fix acoustics, visual access and planned aids; refer to appropriate educational specialist for modality equivalence. | Fresh evaluation with the same cognitive criteria; document permitted adaptation. |
| Speech understood despite nonstandard accent | Avoid ungrounded correction of accent; ask listener only about *actual misunderstanding*. | Listener independently paraphrases the point; no arbitrary accent scale. |

## Transfer T1 — Community noticeboard (public information)

**Fictional setting:** A community centre could place either a map or an events calendar by the entrance. A partner wants visitors to find information quickly.

**Fixed partner prompts:** “What should visitors see first?” and after the learner's question, “How would your idea help someone new?”

**Task in English:** “Discuss one option with your partner. Answer both questions, ask one relevant question, and then briefly present your proposal with a reason and an example.”

**Acceptable production (not the only answer):** “I would add a map. Which rooms do visitors usually need?” / “It would help someone find the right room quickly.” Presentation: “A map would help new visitors because the building has several rooms. For example, a visitor could use it to find the art workshop.” **Rubric:** relevant answer, real turn-taking, proposal-reason-example, autonomous intelligible delivery. **Common error:** learner describes a map only, with no reason; prompt after evaluation “Explain how a map would help a visitor.”

## Transfer T2 — Eco-friendly school exhibition (different context)

**Fictional setting:** A class exhibition needs one display: reused-material sculptures or a board explaining ways to reduce paper waste. Do not judge actual household habits or moral worth.

**Fixed partner prompts:** “Which display could teach visitors something?” and “What could visitors learn from it?” after the learner's question.

**Task in English:** “Respond to the partner's choice, ask a related question, and then give a short, clear presentation of your idea, reason and example.”

**One acceptable response:** “I would show reused-material sculptures. What would you choose?” / “They could see how old boxes become something new.” Presentation: “I recommend the sculptures because they show a creative way to reuse materials. For example, students could turn clean boxes into a model town.” **Rubric:** response, related question, contingent second answer, meaningful reason and concrete example; either option is valid when defended. **Typical misconception:** saying “My option is green” with no example does not yet show a reasoned account.

## Delayed recall

**Next session (editorial suggestion):** Without a sample dialogue, ask learners to identify `turn-taking / idea / reason / example` from four short spoken lines. Then engage in one 4-turn exchange using a new school-related question and give a single reason.

**Several sessions later:** Partner asks, “How could we make a fictional games room easier to share?” Learner prepares briefly, then holds a new 4-turn interaction and gives idea-reason-example without leading frames. Record which component transfers; if one fails, remediate that component rather than restarting the entire course. This delayed recall is separate from mastery items 009 and 010.

## Professional limits

Do not make learners disclose personal experiences. Different Englishes and accents are legitimate; assess listener comprehension and relevance. For speech/hearing differences, adaptations may change the evidence available for an **oral** objective, so document a human-equivalence decision before using the adjusted modality to establish formal mastery. Audio/video capture is not required.
