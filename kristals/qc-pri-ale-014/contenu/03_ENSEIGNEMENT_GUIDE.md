# 03 — Enseignement guidé et modelage | `qc-pri-ale-014`

## Announce the target

“Today we will listen, respond to a partner, ask a related question, and explain an idea using a reason and an example. We do not all have to agree.”

## Step 1 — Listen for the purpose of the question

The teacher asks, “What would improve our class library?” The learner identifies the **topic** (class library) and **action required** (suggest an improvement). If a message is unclear, model “Could you say that again, please?” as a valid listening-repair move.

**Show two responses:**
- Related: “We could make a small book display.”
- Unrelated: “My shoes are blue.”

The learner explains why only the first response fits the partner's question; this oral comprehension check is not yet proof of the whole mastery target.

## Step 2 — Model turn-taking without a rigid script

1. **Teacher:** “Would you choose a book display or a reading seat?”
2. **Model learner:** “I would choose a reading seat. What would you choose?”
3. **Teacher:** “A book display. Why do you prefer the seat?”
4. **Model learner:** “A seat would give readers a quieter place. A display could also help readers find books.”

**Think-aloud:** “I listened to the question and answered it. I then asked a related question and waited. In my second answer I responded to the partner's *why*, rather than repeating my first answer.” The teacher can switch the choice and reasoning in a new practice, demonstrating flexibility instead of memorization.

## Step 3 — Model a mini-presentation with evidence

**Visual template:** `MY IDEA` → `REASON` → `CONCRETE EXAMPLE`.

**Teacher's model in English:** “I suggest a small book display because students could discover stories they have not read. For example, every Friday we could feature three different books at the entrance.”

**Label aloud:** “My idea: a display. My reason: discover stories. My example: three featured books on Fridays.” A reason is not a slogan, and the example must show a possible action.

## Step 4 — Counter-model and repair

**Not sufficient:** “Books. Books are nice. Nice books!” → Does not show response to a question, relevant reason or specific example.

**Repair:** “I suggest a book display because students can find new stories. For example, each student could recommend one book for the display.”

**Not sufficient:** a learner reads a long prepared paragraph while the partner's question changes. → Strong delivery alone is not evidence of **interaction**. **Repair:** answer the new question first, let the partner respond and then present an argument.

## Step 5 — Check understanding and separate components

Ask: “What did your partner ask you? How did your reply connect to the question? What is your reason? Give one specific example.” Learner identifies each component from the model and gives one fresh idea. Check spoken meaning, not an imposed accent or performance persona. Teacher then transfers to `contenu/04_PRATIQUE_ACCOMPAGNEE.md` with progressively less help.

**Accessibility and safeguarding:** Maintain quiet acoustic conditions; position the partner so face and speech are accessible. Instructions may be clarified in French before the practice; allow a brief planning card. No claim that a written paragraph automatically proves the oral target. Never require disclosure of real personal circumstances.
