# 05 — Four independent rehearsals | `qc-pri-ale-014`

These four activities prepare the learner but **do not replace** the two mastery contexts in items 009 and 010. They are intended for fresh oral responses rather than memorized scripts. The partner uses the specified line, listens, then asks the specified follow-up. The teacher observes without supplying the content. A brief `idea → reason → example` blank organizer is allowed if agreed in advance. Example productions are for evaluators **after** the attempt.

## A1 — Choose a creative club

**Partner:** “Which club should our fictional school offer: drawing or storytelling?” **Learner:** answer, ask a related question. **Partner follow-up:** “How would students take part?” **Learner:** answer, then present a choice with reason and example.

**Possible acceptable production:** “I suggest storytelling. Would you join that club?” / “Students could invent stories in pairs.” / “A storytelling club would let students share ideas. For example, pairs could create short stories and read them aloud.” **Criteria:** follows the question, makes space for partner, explains a benefit and illustrates it; different club choice valid.

## A2 — Improve an imaginary library sign

**Partner:** “Our fictional library has a confusing sign. What should we do?” **Learner:** reply and ask a related question. **Partner follow-up:** “How would your idea help new visitors?” **Learner:** respond, then present idea-reason-example.

**Possible response:** “We could add clear pictures to the sign. Which room is hardest to find?” / “Pictures would help visitors recognize rooms. For example, a book symbol could point to the reading room.” **Criteria:** coherent connection between sign, visitors, reason, and example. **Feedback:** If no question is asked, retry with a new signage scenario, not a teacher-supplied sentence.

## A3 — Respectfully answer a different view

**Partner:** “I think we should paint one large mural at our fictional school. What do you think?” **Learner:** express agreement or disagreement respectfully and ask why partner prefers the mural. **Partner follow-up:** “Because everyone could contribute. What is one reason for your idea?” **Learner:** respond and present choice with one concrete example.

**Possible response:** “That could work. I would also add small paintings. Why do you prefer one mural?” / “Small paintings could let groups try different ideas. For example, each group could paint a different season.” **Criteria:** listening, respectful follow-up, genuine reason and example. Agreeing with the mural is equally acceptable.

## A4 — Announce a fictional class project

**Partner:** “What short project could our class do next week?” **Learner:** propose project and ask about a practical concern. **Partner follow-up:** “What would we need to begin?” **Learner:** respond, then give a compact presentation with an example.

**Possible response:** “We could make paper bookmarks. Would we have enough paper?” / “We could use paper and colouring pencils.” Presentation: “Bookmark making is a good class project because everyone can make something useful. For example, students could create bookmarks for the classroom library.” **Criteria:** responds to partner question, clear proposal, plausible materials, reason and example.

## Evaluator record and self-check

For A1–A4 record: partner question heard → relevant answer → learner's own related question → response to follow-up → exact words of idea/reason/example → adaptations and any directional assistance. A valid performance need not sound like the sample. A clear alternative reason and different appropriate example are accepted, but a generic claim (“It is good because it is good”) does not demonstrate reasoning. The learner can ask for repetition (“Could you repeat that?”), which is an interactive skill rather than an automatic penalty.
