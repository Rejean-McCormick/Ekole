# 02 — Diagnostic, sans blocage artificiel | `qc-pri-ale-014`

## Précondition et accès

`requires`: `qc-pri-ale-013` (*Écouter et comprendre des échanges*). La démonstration d'une compréhension équivalente remplit la condition sans obligation de terminer ce Kristal préalable. Aucun ordre conseillé. Décrire oralement les tâches en anglais; expliciter la procédure en français au besoin. L'évaluation distingue **écoute**, **interaction**, **présentation** et non seulement lecture ou écriture.

## Diagnostic D1 — Listening and contingent response (minimum 2 interlocutors)

**Say once, clearly:** “I like drawing on rainy days. What activity do you enjoy when it rains?”

**Learner's tasks:** (a) say in English what activity the partner likes; (b) answer the question with a plausible activity; (c) ask a relevant question in English about drawing or rainy-day activities.

**Possible acceptable response:** “You like drawing. I enjoy puzzles on rainy days. What do you like to draw?”

**Observable checks:** accurate identification of *drawing*; reply refers to the question, not an unrelated favourite animal; question invites an actual partner response. Do not require the learner to reveal a true preference — fictional choices are welcome. **If the learner cannot identify the partner's idea:** check auditory access and replay after this diagnostic, then reassess comprehension with a new, fixed utterance.

## Diagnostic D2 — Idea, reason, example and a real follow-up

**Partner asks:** “Would you add a music corner to our imaginary classroom?” **Learner** offers an idea, reason and example orally, then invites or responds to one further question. The partner's fixed follow-up is: “How would students use it?”

**Possible acceptable response:** “Yes. A music corner could help students explore sounds. For example, they could use small percussion instruments at a planned time. What do you think?” Following the partner's question: “They could take turns trying the instruments.”

**Checks:** idea is identifiable; `because` or an equivalent explanation makes the reason coherent; example includes concrete action; learner responds to the follow-up. No single personal opinion is required.

## Decision grid (formative)

| What was actually seen | Next step |
|---|---|
| D1 listening secure, D2 already shows reason + example and actual exchange | Offer shorter teaching then a different autonomous activity; do not hold the learner behind a needless gate. |
| D1 listening insecure despite appropriate access | Revisit listening comprehension linked to `qc-pri-ale-013` or give equivalent micro-remediation; retest with a new prompt. |
| Listening understood, but exchange is monologue | Teach turn boundaries and a relevant follow-up (G1/G2); remove cue cards gradually. |
| Dialogue adequate, presentation has no meaningful reason or example | Teach idea–reason–example using original scenarios and counterexamples. |
| Evidence prevented by hearing, speech or participation barrier | Provide planned adaptations and qualified review; record “non concluant” rather than “non maîtrisé”. |

**Trace:** exact partner question; learner's words or faithful observation note; turn order; listening comprehension; possible access supports. No audio recording required.
