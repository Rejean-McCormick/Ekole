# 04 — Three scaffolded practices with fading support | `qc-pri-ale-014`

**Shared observable rubric:** Learner (1) listens and gives a relevant answer; (2) alternates turns, asks a question and responds to the partner's next question; (3) gives a coherent idea with a relevant reason; (4) provides an illustrating example; (5) speaks intelligibly and with decreasing directive assistance. Fictional choices are valid. Do not score accent resemblance.

## G1 — Choosing a quiet activity (high support)

**Scenario:** An imaginary class can choose one rainy-day activity: drawing or puzzles.

**Before first attempt, learner can view this completed cue strip:**
- Partner: “Which activity should we choose?”
- Learner frame: “I suggest ____. What would you choose?”
- Partner: “I would choose drawing. Why do you suggest yours?”
- Learner frame: “I suggest it because ____. For example, ____.”

**Task:** Complete and speak the two learner turns, then add a short presentation to the group using `idea → reason → example`.

**Possible response:** “I suggest puzzles. What would you choose?” / “They help groups solve a problem together. For example, four students could share one puzzle.” / “Our class could try puzzles because students can work together. For example, each group could complete a different puzzle.”

**Fade:** For second rehearsal show only the 4 roles, not completed sentence frames. **Feedback:** If partner choice is ignored, prompt after the rehearsal: “What did the partner choose? Mention it or answer the next question.”

## G2 — Planning a fictional classroom corner (medium support)

**Scenario:** Choose between a plant display and a small science shelf. There is room for one.

**Available prompts before first rehearsal only:** `answer → ask → listen → respond`; `idea → reason → example`. No suggested wording.

- Partner turn 1: “Which corner would you add, and why?”
- Learner turn 2: answer and ask a question about the alternative.
- Partner turn 3: “I would choose a plant display. What could students learn from your choice?”
- Learner turn 4: react to *plant display* and answer what students could learn.
- Then a mini-presentation for a separate audience.

**Possible response:** “I suggest a science shelf because students could explore objects. Would you use it?” / “The plant display is interesting too. With the shelf, we could compare safe objects, such as stones, with labels.” Presentation: “I recommend a small science shelf because it could support observation. For example, students could compare three labelled rocks.”

**Fade:** Hide both cue cards for the second attempt, keeping only the fictional scenario. **Feedback:** “You gave an example, but why does that example support your choice?” Encourage `benefit → illustration`.

## G3 — Organizing an imagination day (low support)

**Scenario:** A class is choosing one activity for a fictional Imagination Day. No sentence starters or sample learner responses are shown.

**Partner fixed lines:** “What activity would you propose?” → after the learner asks a related question: “I would choose story writing. How would your activity include more people?”

**Task:** Learner replies and asks a related question, listens to the response, replies with an inclusive example, and then presents the idea, reason, and specific example to a small audience. Teacher only provides the task and neutral time signal; no cueing during the exchange.

**One post-task evaluator example, not to model beforehand:** Learner proposes group drawing, asks “Which activity would you choose?”, then responds to the partner's choice and explains: “I would choose group drawing because everyone could add ideas. For example, four students could create one picture together.”

**Feedback:** Describe exact observed evidence (“You answered why your activity included people by describing four students drawing together”) or specific omission (“Your reason was clear, but you did not invite the partner into the conversation”). **If assistance was necessary:** train and retry in a new scenario; do not count coached practice as an independent mastery proof.
